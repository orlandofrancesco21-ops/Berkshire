# Memory semiconductors — evaluation framework

> Personal playbook for evaluating businesses whose economics are set by the price of a fungible bit: DRAM (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, CXMT), NAND flash (Samsung, Kioxia/SanDisk, SK hynix/Solidigm, Micron, YMTC), the HBM sub-market that now sits on top of DRAM, and the module/channel and HDD-substitute adjacencies. Written 2026-07-30, at what may or may not turn out to be a cycle peak — read the dated figures as calibration, not as current state.
> Append-only. Refine every time you study a new name in this space.

**Last updated:** 2026-07-30
**Umbrella framework:** no — decision-grade for a single memory maker; the Sub-sector navigator below is triage context, not a sub-framework requirement.

---

## What this industry is

Memory makers sell storage capacity measured in bits, and essentially nothing else. A DRAM die from Micron and a DRAM die from SK hynix at the same speed grade are interchangeable in the buyer's socket; a NAND wafer from Kioxia and one from YMTC hold the same data. The output is genuinely undifferentiated, so the price is set industry-wide by the balance between how many bits the industry can make and how many the world wants — and because a fab is a step function of capacity that takes two to three years to build and cannot be un-built, that balance overshoots in both directions, forever. Revenue is therefore **price × bits**, and in the up-leg it is almost entirely price: SanDisk's quarter ended 2026-04-03 grew revenue 251% year over year on ASP per gigabyte up 248% with **total exabytes shipped flat** (SNDK 10-Q, filed 2026-05-01, verified). Demand comes from four places with very different elasticities — AI accelerators and AI-inference servers (capex-funded, currently price-insensitive), conventional servers, PCs and smartphones (unit-bound, visibly price-elastic — smartphone production is forecast down 15-20% year over year in 2026 and notebooks down ~10% *because memory got expensive*, TrendForce 2026-07-21, partial), and consumer retail (the first to refuse). Supply comes from six-to-nine companies, three of which are state-adjacent and one of which does not need a return on capital. The economics are spectacular at the top — 78-86% gross margins across the complex in mid-2026 — and catastrophic at the bottom: Micron printed a **-32.7% quarterly gross margin** and burned $5.45bn of free cash flow in FY2023 (Micron FY2023 disclosures via secondary recaps, partial), and SanDisk posted GAAP operating losses in FY2023, FY2024 *and* FY2025 (SNDK 10-K FY2025 filed 2025-08-21 and SEC XBRL `OperatingIncomeLoss`, verified). Nine severe boom-bust cycles in about forty years is the industry's own count.

**The one sentence that matters:** DRAM and NAND share a name, a customer list and a set of tool vendors, and almost nothing that determines a stock-picker's outcome. Treating "memory" as one industry is the single most expensive analytical error available in this space, and it is the error the market is making as of this writing.

## Sub-sector navigator

Triage what you are actually looking at **before** applying any multiple. Most memory companies are a portfolio of a good business and a bad one, and the mix ratio is the thesis.

| Sub-sector | What it is | Primary KPI | Primary multiple | Cyclicality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DRAM — commodity** | DDR5/LPDDR for servers, PCs, phones. Three real players (~90% share), EUV-constrained, HBM cannibalises its own wafer supply | Bit supply growth vs bit demand growth; sufficiency ratio | P/B against *forward* book | Violent, but structurally the best-supported memory market in 2026 |
| **DRAM — HBM** | Stacked DRAM sold into AI accelerators; qualified per-customer, multi-year contracted, consumes 3-4x the wafer area per bit | HBM share of the maker's DRAM wafer input; qualification wins per accelerator generation | P/B, cross-checked on contracted revenue | Lower so far — but unproven through a down-leg |
| **NAND — commodity** | Client SSD, embedded, retail cards/USB. Six-plus players, no lithography chokepoint, state-funded entrant | Cost per bit vs the marginal producer; exabytes vs ASP/GB | P/B against forward book, floored on replacement cost | The worst of the two. Structurally over-supplied across cycles |
| **NAND — enterprise SSD** | High-capacity SSD into hyperscalers and AI inference. Overtook consumer in total NAND market value in 2026 (TrendForce, partial) | Datacenter revenue mix and *exabyte* growth (not revenue growth) | Same as commodity NAND, with a mix premium argued not assumed | Better mix, same supply physics. A mix shift, not a moat |
| **Module & channel** | Kingston-type assemblers buying die and selling modules | Inventory turns; gross margin per module | EV/EBITDA or P/E — genuinely different economics | Inverse-ish: they are long inventory into a rising market and short it into a falling one |
| **HDD (substitute)** | Nearline hard disk (STX, WDC post-split). Two-player oligopoly, no fab, benefits when NAND is scarce | Exabytes shipped; nearline mix | Forward P/E works here — the market capitalises HDD earnings at 3-5x the multiple it gives memory earnings | Cyclical but far shallower; supply adds are incremental, not step-function |
| **Picks and shovels** | ASML.AS, Lam, Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron | Memory share of bookings; WFE spend | Forward P/E / EV/EBITDA | Same cycle, one derivative removed — they sell into the capex response rather than the price |

## Industry structure

- **Concentration:** **two different answers, and this is the whole framework.** DRAM: three real players — Samsung ~38.5%, SK hynix ~28.8%, Micron ~22.4% of Q1 2026 revenue, roughly 90% in three hands (TrendForce/Omdia via trade reports, 2026-06, partial), with CXMT emerging. NAND: six-plus — Samsung ~29%, SK hynix/Solidigm ~14%, Kioxia ~14%, YMTC ~13%, Micron ~13%, SanDisk ~13% (Counterpoint/TrendForce via trade reports, 2026, partial), so the top three sum to ~57%. There is no share level at which a NAND player can behave as a price-setter and three at which a DRAM player can.
- **Customer:** B2B, concentrated but rarely above the 10% disclosure line for any single account. SanDisk reported **no customer above 10% of revenue** in FY2025 or FY2024, top ten at 40% (SNDK 10-K FY2025, verified). Hyperscalers now contract multi-year, which changes the risk shape (see "Red flags").
- **Cyclicality:** extreme, and the amplitude is the point. Peak-to-trough in the fundamentals has run **6-8 quarters** across the 2016-19 and 2020-23 cycles. Micron revenue fell **49.5%** in one fiscal year (FY2022 $30.76bn → FY2023 $15.54bn, company releases, verified). Drawdowns in the equity, computed from split/dividend-adjusted closes for MU back to 1990: **-81.7%** (1995-09 → 1996-07), **-98.2%** (2000-07 → 2008-11), -49.6% (2022), -57.6% (2024-06 → 2025-04). MU's July 2000 peak was not closed above again until **2022-01-04 — 21.5 years** (computed from yfinance adjusted closes, 2026-07-30, verified). Size positions against that number, not against the 6-8 quarter operating cycle.
- **Capital intensity:** asset-heavy in economics, sometimes asset-light on the balance sheet — a trap. A greenfield DRAM fab is **$15-20bn+ and 2-3 years** for 100-200k wafer starts/month (trade/analyst estimates, unverified). Where a maker participates through a JV, its reported PP&E capex can be trivial while its real commitment is enormous: SanDisk's own 9M FY2026 capex was **$134M** against **$6,543M of Flash Ventures commitments** plus $993M of lease guarantees (SNDK 10-Q q/e 2026-04-03, verified).
- **Operating leverage:** the highest of any industry I have a framework for, and symmetric. Fixed costs are shared or absorbed regardless of output. SanDisk moved from 22.5% gross margin (quarter ended 2025-03-28) to 78.4% (quarter ended 2026-04-03) in five quarters (SNDK filings, verified). The same mechanism runs backwards at identical speed.
- **Regulatory regime:** heavy and getting heavier, but as *industrial policy*, not as product regulation. Export controls, subsidised national champions (CXMT, YMTC), CHIPS-style domestic-fab incentives, and customer qualification of Chinese parts are the live variables. The regulatory question in memory is never "will they be allowed to sell this" — it is "who is being paid to add supply".
- **Geographic concentration:** production is overwhelmingly Korea, Japan, Taiwan, China and (increasingly) the US; demand is global but the marginal buyer is a US hyperscaler. Concentration risk is physical (one Yokkaichi power event moves world prices) and geopolitical.

## What makes a great business in this industry

Ranked by how much I think each actually explains through-cycle returns. Note how few of these are the traits that make a great business anywhere else.

1. **DRAM weighting.** The highest-explanatory-power variable in the industry, and it is a mix question, not a quality question. The cleanest evidence available: through the same trough and the same recovery, the DRAM-weighted operator was back at record profit while the pure-NAND operator was still losing money two years later — Micron net income -$5.83bn (FY2023) → +$0.778bn (FY2024) → +$8.54bn (FY2025), company releases, verified; SanDisk GAAP operating income -$2,035M → -$468M → -$1,377M over the corresponding years, SEC filings, verified. Caveat honestly: n=2, one cycle, mismatched fiscal calendars, and SanDisk carried spin-off and impairment noise. It is illustration, not proof, and building the real series (segment ROIC for DRAM vs NAND across 2010-2026) needs paid data the vault does not have. **This is the framework's central claim and it is well-reasoned rather than quantitatively proven. Say so in any thesis that leans on it.**
2. **Cost per bit relative to the industry's marginal producer — rank, not level.** The only question at the trough is whether you are still above water when the highest-cost player is not. Technology leadership (layer count, node transition, hybrid bonding) matters exactly and only insofar as it converts into cost-per-bit rank. If you cannot state where the company sits on that curve, you do not yet have a view on quality.
3. **Product mix toward where the buyer cannot substitute.** HBM > enterprise SSD > server DRAM > mobile > PC > retail channel. The buyer's substitution options, not the seller's technology, set the durability of the price.
4. **A balance sheet that funds a -33% gross-margin year without a dilutive raise.** Not leverage at the peak — *quarters of trough cash burn funded by the current cash pile*. Micron burned $5.45bn of FCF in FY2023 (partial). The industry currently enters its peak with net cash, which is genuinely different from prior cycles and is the strongest single point in the bull case.
5. **Absence of a fixed-cost obligation that cannot be switched off.** This is where JV structures fail the test. SanDisk holds 49.9% of the three Flash Ventures entities, is entitled to ~50% of output at cost-plus-a-small-markup, **must pay half of all fixed costs regardless of how much output it takes**, must fund ~50% of the JV's capital investment when JV operating cash flow is short, and is contractually **barred from manufacturing flash itself** (SNDK 10-Q q/e 2026-04-03 Note 10 and S-3ASR 2026-02-17, verified). Read as an owner: a JV partner in a commodity cannot cut its own fixed costs in a downturn, and cannot build its way out. In January 2026 it paid Kioxia **$1.2bn over 2026-2029** to extend that arrangement to 2034-12-31 (same source, verified).
6. **Genuine capital discipline, evidenced across a full cycle rather than stated.** Almost every memory management claims counter-cyclicality; the observable is capex by year against the price level at which it was committed.
7. **A management that sells forward at the right point of the cycle.** Multi-year customer agreements are new to this industry as of 2026 and are the most interesting structural development in it. Selling forward at the top trades peak upside for trough survival — that can be the correct trade, but it must be *priced* as the trade it is, not celebrated as backlog.

## Key drivers of returns

What makes the **stock** work over a 5-year hold. Note that the first four are industry variables, not company variables — this is the industry where the company matters least.

- **Where you bought relative to mid-cycle price.** Dominant. Everything else is second order. The industry's own history says entry at a peak multiple of book has cost years-to-decades, not quarters.
- **The direction of the supply/demand sufficiency ratio in the product you are exposed to.** A 4-5% deficit produced 90%+ quarterly contract price increases in early 2026. The industry's P&L is a derivative of a small number, so a small forecast error either side of the line produces an enormous P&L error.
- **The second derivative of contract price, not the level.** Memory equities discount the rate of change. NAND contract prices QoQ ran +55-60% (1Q26) → +70-75% (2Q26) → +10-15% forecast (3Q26); DRAM ran +93-98% (1Q26) → +58-63% (2Q26) → +13-18% forecast (3Q26) (TrendForce releases, 2026-07-03 and 2026-07-09, partial-to-verified). Prices were still rising when the complex fell 30-56% from its June 2026 high. That is not irrational; it is the market pricing the derivative it actually trades.
- **Whether the capex committed at peak margin lands into a market that still wants it.** The fuse is 2-3 years to first wafer, 3-4 to meaningful bits. Today's announcements are the oversupply of 2028-2029; today's price is being set by decisions taken in 2023-2024.
- **Cost-per-bit progress relative to ASP decline in the down-leg.** This *is* memory gross margin. A maker whose cost falls faster than the price survives the trough with the option to buy assets; one whose doesn't, dilutes.
- **Book value compounding at the peak.** Underrated and mechanical: a peak year can add more to book value than the entire pre-existing book, which resets every asset-based valuation anchor within twelve months. See "Valuation framework".
- **Whether the multi-year contracted book holds its price when spot rolls.** Untested. No NAND or DRAM long-term agreement has ever been stress-tested through a collapse. This is the single highest-value unknown in the industry as of 2026-07-30.

## Industry-specific KPIs

Generic financial ratios are close to useless here, and P/E is worse than useless (see "Valuation framework"). Note that the two best leading series are **third-party data the company does not disclose** — which is a structural weakness of any monitoring trigger built on them.

| Metric | What good looks like | What concerning looks like | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bit supply growth vs bit demand growth**, by product | Demand exceeds supply by 3pp+ | Supply exceeds demand by 2pp+ | The single causal variable in the industry. DRAM 2026: demand mid-30s% vs 16% supply (IDC via commentary, partial) |
| **Sufficiency ratio** (TrendForce's supply/demand balance) | Negative (deficit) and widening | Positive, or negative and narrowing | As of 2026-07-30: DRAM -1% to -2% for 2026 and widening into 2027; **NAND turns positive in 2H27** (TrendForce 2026-07-21 and 2026-07-30, verified). The divergence *is* the DRAM/NAND thesis |
| **Contract-price momentum (2nd derivative)** | Accelerating or stable | Decelerating two quarters running | Absolute price peaks have historically landed 2-4 quarters after momentum peaks. Momentum peaked in Q1 2026 |
| **Exabytes shipped vs ASP per GB**, decomposed | Both rising, or bits rising faster | Revenue rising on price alone with bits flat or falling | The most honest single disclosure in a memory income statement, and one of the few the companies actually give. Flat bits + 3.5x price is a peak-cycle P&L by definition |
| **Inventory weeks — supplier and OEM/distributor, separately** | Supplier 3-5 weeks with OEM 2-4 | Supplier fine but **OEM/distributor 7-9+ and rising** | The channel builds before the maker does. ~8 weeks is "normal"; the post-COVID glut peaked above 31 weeks (commentary, unverified). The customer-side number is the leading one and it is the one nobody quotes |
| **Cost per bit decline vs ASP per bit decline** | Cost falling faster | ASP falling through cost | This *is* gross margin. **No supplier discloses either in absolute terms** — only sporadic QoQ percentages. Any trigger keyed to cost-per-bit needs a named proxy (gross margin at a stated ASP change) or it is unusable |
| **Capex measured against MID-CYCLE revenue** | 25-35% of mid-cycle revenue | Above 40%, or rising while price momentum decelerates | Capex/sales lies harder at the peak than any other memory metric, because the denominator is a bubble. A quarter that looks like a benign 17% of sales can be ~19% *per quarter* against the prior fiscal year's revenue |
| **Industry capex in absolute dollars, by supplier, YoY** | Flat or down at peak margin | Up 20%+ at peak margin | The oldest tell in the industry. SK hynix guided 2026 capex **+50% to at least $31bn** on 2026-07-29 with an operating margin above 80% (Bloomberg/trade press, partial) — and the complex sold off |
| **HBM share of the maker's DRAM wafer input** | Rising | Falling | The supply-destruction valve: ~19% of DRAM wafer input in 2025 → ~22-23% in 2026e (TrendForce via trade reports, partial). Every point of HBM share removes more than a point of conventional bit supply |
| **Chinese share of bit output, by product** | Flat | Stepping up, or a tier-1 customer qualifying a Chinese part | NAND: ~19% of global bit output by 2027 (TrendForce 2026-07-21, verified). CXMT expected at ~350k WSPM by end-2026, near Micron's DRAM capacity (trade press, unverified) |
| **Fab utilisation, and under-utilisation charges in the notes** | ~95-100%, no charges | Below 70%; 50% is the floor observed in real downturns | Fixed-cost absorption is the entire margin story. Under-utilisation charges appear in the notes before they appear in the margin |
| **Quarters of trough cash burn funded by current cash** | 8+ quarters at a -30% gross margin | Under 4, or a JV/take-or-pay obligation that runs regardless | The only balance-sheet test that matters. Not leverage at the peak |
| **Contracted share of next-year bits, and the price mechanic** | Rising share, with a disclosed floor or index | Rising share with **volume-only** commitments, or price mechanics not disclosed | A volume commitment at spot price is not a floor. See "Red flags" |

## Valuation framework

**P/E has no valid domain in this industry.** At the trough the denominator is negative and the ratio does not exist; at the peak a mid-single-digit forward P/E is the *signature of the top*, not a valuation argument. As of 2026-07-30, SNDK trades at 5.87x forward earnings and MU at 5.56x, with SK hynix around 5.2x and Samsung around 5-7x (SNDK/MU from yfinance 2026-07-30, verified; the Korean names from commentary, unverified). That is not six independent cheapness signals — it is one consensus forecast of one peak year, quoted six times. Micron peaked in May 2018 at a forward P/E of roughly 4-5x (commentary, unverified) and then fell ~56% *while earnings were still rising*.

- **Primary multiple: P/B — but against a forward book, not the reported one.** This is the non-obvious point and it is the most useful thing in this file. Book value compounds violently at the peak, so trailing P/B is a stale denominator in the *opposite* direction from the usual trap. Worked example, 2026-07-30: SNDK reported book value per share of $93.09 → P/B 13.42x. Add consensus Q4 FY26 EPS of $34.52 → BVPS ~$127.60, P/B 9.79x. Add consensus FY2027 EPS of $212.95 → BVPS ~$340.55, **P/B 3.67x** (computed from yfinance BVPS and the 21-analyst consensus table, 2026-07-30, verified). The company would earn 2.3x its entire current book in one year. So 13.4x book is no more evidence of overvaluation than 0.81x was evidence of cheapness in 2016 — both are stale denominators. Anchor on the post-peak-year book, and state plainly that the anchor only exists if the peak year is delivered.
- **Secondary cross-checks (three genuinely independent lenses, not one lens counted three times):** (a) **EV/replacement cost** — a greenfield DRAM fab at $15-20bn+ for 100-200k WSPM gives an unambiguous rebuild cost for installed capacity, and the market has repeatedly priced incumbents below it at troughs; it is also the bear anchor at the top. (b) **Mid-cycle normalized earnings** — run in *both* directions here: at the trough normalise up, at the peak normalise **down**, and write down what "normal" gross margin is assumed to be, because that assumption is the valuation. The historically sustainable commodity-DRAM band is cited at 55-70% against 84.9-86% printed in mid-2026 (commentary, unverified) — and a NAND-only business's normal is lower still. (c) **What the current price implies about the out-year**, solved backwards. A DCF, an FCF yield and an EBITDA multiple all driven off one bit-price forecast are one lens wearing three hats.
- **Historical range:** the only long clean series available is Micron's P/B — troughs around **0.81x (2016-02), 0.97x (2019-05), 1.32x (2023-02)** with a 10-year median near 1.93x (macrotrends via search summary; the source returned HTTP 403 and this could not be read directly — **unverified**, and it is doing real work in the anchors above, so treat the magnitude as the signal and the decimals as unsupported). **Working rule, provisional and minted here from one name's history:** sub-1.2x book is where memory has historically been buyable; above ~3x book you are underwriting a regime change; above 10x you are not doing valuation. Replace this the first time a second name's full-cycle P/B series is assembled.
- **What drives the multiple up vs down:** the sufficiency ratio and its direction; contract-price momentum; announced industry capex; Chinese capacity news; the HBM/commodity mix; and — new in this cycle — the credibility of long-term customer agreements. Company-specific execution moves the multiple far less than in any other framework here.
- **When does the multiple lie?** Constantly, and in a specific pattern. (a) **Forward P/E at the peak** — lowest at the top, undefined at the bottom, inverted throughout. (b) **Trailing anything, either side of an inflection** — a maker whose TTM still contains two loss quarters looks expensive on EV/EBITDA at exactly the moment its run-rate has re-based; SNDK's headline 32x TTM EV/EBITDA is ~10.9x on annualised most-recent-quarter EBITDA (computed 2026-07-30, verified). (c) **P/B at the peak**, per the forward-book point above. (d) **Vendor-supplied enterprise value** — yfinance's EV field was understated for all four memory/HDD names checked on 2026-07-30 (SNDK $146.9bn reported vs ~$181bn reconciled from market cap + debt − cash; MU $815bn vs ~$945bn), so every EV multiple derived from it was ~24% too low. Reconcile EV by hand before quoting any EV multiple in this industry. (e) **Reported "operating income" from data vendors** — for a JV participant, vendor normalisation can differ from the filed GAAP line by more than a billion dollars a year. Pull `OperatingIncomeLoss` from SEC XBRL or read the statement.

## Red flags specific to this industry

Things that look fine in a generic financial scan and signal real trouble here.

- **Capex guidance raised at record margin.** The industry's oldest tell and the one that fires first. Words are cheap and the latest budget at the latest peak is the test — credit claimed discipline only against a full cycle of actual capex-by-year data, never against the IR deck (see CAPA-P2). SK hynix on 2026-07-29: operating profit +550% YoY, margin above 80%, capex guided +50% to ≥$31bn; the stock fell. When management is *simultaneously* the evidence for the shortage and the cause of the glut, believe the capex line.
- **Gross margin materially above the historically sustainable band.** 84.9-86% against a cited 55-70% sustainable band and a ~60% prior-cycle peak (commentary, unverified) has never been a starting condition for a good entry. And a margin this far above precedent invites the trap of reading the margin as evidence of a moat — delete every trailing metric from the argument and see whether a moat is still described (see MOAT-T1). In memory it will not be: there is no mechanism, only a price.
- **Revenue growth that is entirely price, with bits flat or falling.** The decomposition is disclosed and almost nobody looks at it. Flat exabytes with ASP/GB up 248% is not growth; it is the same business charging 3.5x. Watch the segment detail too — bits can be growing in datacenter while falling 10% in client and 40% in consumer, which is the seller reallocating a fixed bit pool to the highest bidder, not winning share.
- **Contract-price momentum decelerating while the narrative still accelerates.** The leading operating series in this industry are contract-price second derivative and OEM/distributor inventory weeks — neither is in the income statement, and by the time the P&L shows the turn the multiple has already moved (see NARC-P2). Both had turned by 2026-07-30 while sell-side FY2027 estimates were still being revised up 6-to-0.
- **Any trigger keyed to a third-party or undisclosed series without a named fallback.** TrendForce sufficiency ratios, contract-price indices and cost-per-bit are not company disclosures; a kill switch keyed to one dies the quarter the vendor changes methodology or the subscription lapses, and a company that stops disclosing exabyte/ASP direction has told you something by the omission (see PROD-T3). Every metric-keyed switch in a memory thesis needs a named proxy written next to it.
- **The customer's own economics breaking on the price.** Samsung's mobile division reportedly posted its **first-ever operating loss** in Q2 2026 because memory got too expensive (secondary reports of the 2026-07-29 release, partial). When the seller's own downstream division cannot absorb the price, the price is not stable — and this is visible a quarter or two before it shows up in the memory maker's order book.
- **A long-term agreement whose price mechanics are not disclosed.** This is the 2026-specific flag and the most important new one. A multi-year agreement can fix volume, fix price, fix a floor, or index — and only one of those is a floor under earnings. SanDisk verifiably disclosed **$41.6bn of remaining performance obligations** at 2026-04-03 with $41.2bn unbilled, only ~15% (~$6.2bn) scheduled into the next twelve months and only $0.4bn actually prepaid (10-Q, verified) — but **whether those contracts fix price, a floor, or volume alone could not be established from any primary document.** Until it is, a backlog headline in this industry is a volume statement, not an earnings floor. Ask the question explicitly on any name that reports one.
- **JV or take-or-pay obligations that cannot be switched off.** Half of fixed costs regardless of output taken is precisely the wrong balance-sheet feature for a commodity trough, and it does not appear anywhere in a leverage screen. Read the related-party note, the guarantee disclosures, and the material-cash-requirements table — not the debt line.
- **"Zero debt" as a claim about obligation.** A net-cash memory maker can still owe billions in JV commitments, lease guarantees, prepaid building depreciation and minimum-purchase agreements. The honest number is committed cash requirements, not net debt.
- **A software or architecture answer to the shortage.** Compression of the demand itself — KV-cache compression for inference is the current candidate, with one reported claim of a ~6x reduction in AI memory footprint (2026-03, **unverified in existence, magnitude and adoption**) — attacks the demand leg directly and looks identical on a screen to ordinary cycle fear. Naming the cause of a multiple compression is what separates the mean-reverting case from the structural one (see VAL-T2); an unverified press claim is not yet a cause, but it is exactly the shape of thing that should be verified rather than ignored.
- **Share gained at the bottom of the price stack.** In a commodity, share taken on price is a cost, not a moat.
- **A buyback authorised at a peak multiple of book.** Grade it by the multiple executed at and by the share count actually retired, never by the dollars announced (see CAPA-P1) — and net it against equity-comp issuance before believing either read (see CAPA-M1). A memory buyback at 13x book is value destruction wearing the label of capital return, and a programme sized at ~3% of market cap of which a meaningful share merely offsets dilution is not a capital-return event at all.

## Common bull-case archetypes

The stories that make investors overpay here. Each has a documented ancestor; the trap is not restating one, it is buying it without naming what killed the same story last cycle.

- **"Capital discipline is real this time."** Killed in 1995-96 (roughly 50 fab plans announced, then DRAM prices -51% and -65%), killed in 2017-18, and being killed in public right now by SK hynix's +50% capex guide, Samsung's P5, Micron's Idaho fabs and CXMT's ramp. The disconfirming observable is absolute capex dollars by supplier, announced at peak margin — and it has already fired.
- **"Consolidation has fixed the industry."** True in DRAM: three players, ~90% share, a hard lithography chokepoint. **False in NAND**, where consolidation kept being announced and not happening while YMTC added a seventh player. Applying the DRAM structure argument to a NAND asset is the most common version of this error, and the fact that DRAM and NAND sufficiency ratios are forecast to *diverge* in 2027 is the cleanest available proof that the structure — not the AI story — is what is load-bearing.
- **"It's a growth business now, not a cyclical — AI changed the demand shape."** The strongest steelman this industry has ever had, and partly true: inference demand is token-bound rather than unit-bound, content per accelerator has scaled ~7x in a few years, DRAM density gains have collapsed to ~2x per decade from ~100x, EUV is a single-vendor chokepoint, and HBM destroys conventional bit supply as it grows. Every one of those arguments also has a 1995 and a 2017 ancestor that was *also* true. The demand story being genuinely better and the stock being a bad buy are orthogonal claims, and the framework must hold both at once.
- **"It's cheap at 6x earnings."** The peak signature dressed as value. A low multiple on peak earnings and a high multiple on trough earnings are the same mistake — see "Mental-model crossover risks" below, where the imported screening habit that carries this error is named.
- **"The demand is contracted, so it's de-risked."** Contracted demand at fixed price is also *capped* demand — long-term agreements with US hyperscalers are the stated reason Q3 2026 server DRAM increases moderate to 13-18% (TrendForce 2026-07-09, verified). A supplier that sold forward into a shortage has traded the top of the cycle for the bottom of it. That may be the right trade; it is not a free one.
- **"The balance sheet is pristine, so the downside is protected."** Net cash protects against dilution and bankruptcy. It does not protect against a 45-78% drawdown, and it says nothing about the multiple you paid.

## Common bear-case archetypes

Which bear arguments are genuine trouble and which are noise.

- **Genuine: the sufficiency ratio flipping positive in your product.** The most reliable single bear signal, and it is forecast rather than observed, which is why it arrives before the price does. NAND's 2H27 crossover was published 2026-07-21 and reaffirmed 2026-07-30.
- **Genuine: OEM and distributor inventory weeks rising while supplier inventory still looks tight.** The channel is where the glut assembles.
- **Genuine: a missed cost-per-bit transition while peers land theirs.** In a business where cost rank is the only moat, one missed node or layer transition is a permanent re-rating, not a bad quarter.
- **Genuine: a tier-1 customer qualifying a Chinese part.** Qualification is the gate; once it opens, share moves in quarters. Reports of Apple testing CXMT parts (late July 2026, **unverified**) are exactly the shape of thing to verify rather than dismiss.
- **Genuine: the buyer's own margins breaking on the input price.** Demand destruction shows up in the customer's P&L first.
- **Noise: spot price wobbles of a few weeks.** Spot leads contract, but the lead is noisy and the vault currently has **no dated spot series at all** — a real gap in this framework's monitoring surface.
- **Noise: a single quarter's share loss.** Share in a commodity oscillates with allocation decisions.
- **Noise: hyperscaler "digestion" headlines while contracted backlog is still rising.** Watch the backlog disclosure, not the commentary.
- **The trap that catches value investors specifically:** a quality memory franchise can look cheap on the way down for years, and compression from a peak rating to a trough one has run three to five years in the cases this vault records — see the sizing bullet in "Mental-model crossover risks" below. Micron's P/B did not reach 0.97x until roughly a full year after the 2018 stock peak.

## Capital allocation patterns

- **The trough instrument in this industry is probably assets, not own stock.** This is a live disagreement in my own principles and memory sits squarely inside it: the general rule that trough repurchase is the highest-return use of cash has a standing dissent from commodity and capital-intensive businesses, where the counter-cyclical move at the bottom is buying capacity cheaply off a balance sheet repaired at the top. Memory has both features — a commodity clearing price the company does not control, and a trough where survival is the binding constraint. My working position: at the trough, prefer the operator buying distressed capacity or funding a node transition over the one retiring shares; at the peak, prefer distribution and balance-sheet repair over repurchase. Note the divergence rather than assuming the generic rule.
- **Buybacks announced at peak margin are the industry's signature value destruction.** Announcement is permission, not purchase; grade on execution price against book. A programme sized at a low single-digit percentage of market cap, launched within weeks of an all-time high, with a meaningful share absorbing equity-comp dilution, is a capital-return press release.
- **Dividends are rare and initiation at the top is a warning, not a maturity signal.** In a business that can print a -33% gross margin, a dividend committed at peak cash flow is a claim on the trough balance sheet.
- **Debt retired early with peak-cycle cash is genuinely good.** It is the one allocation move that is unambiguously correct at this point in the cycle, and it is cheap to verify.
- **Watch the discretionary strategic stake.** A minority equity position in another maker, bought at the top of your own cycle, locked up for years, is the memory-industry version of the peak-cycle acquisition — it may be a smart supply-chain hedge and it may be capital spent because it was there. Reasonable people will disagree; a thesis should say which case it is underwriting rather than assume the flattering one.
- **The absence of an equity raise during a huge run is a genuine governance credit** — but check whether it was a choice or a constraint. Post-spin tax-matters agreements routinely restrict share issuance for a period; "did not dilute" and "could not dilute" are different facts.
- **Insider behaviour reads differently at a peak.** A founder-scale holding never sold is real alignment; second-tier trimming into strength is not a signal. But note that performance-share plans designed before a commodity spike can have their entire dynamic range consumed by the price move, after which they provide zero incremental incentive — worth checking the payout ladder against the current price before crediting alignment to plan design.

## Mental-model crossover risks

The assumptions from my other frameworks that **do not transfer here**. My existing frameworks are creative software, branded athletic footwear, managed care and energy — three of the four are demand-side, brand or regulatory businesses, and none is a pure commodity manufacturer. Most of my quality-investing instincts are actively wrong in this industry, and several of them fail in the *opposite* direction from where they usually fail.

- **Switching costs do not exist here.** Crediting lock-in requires that leaving strands an asset the *customer* built (see MOAT-P1). A DRAM die strands nothing — no archive, no format, no history. Qualification cycles delay substitution by quarters, not years, which is why a Chinese part passing qualification at one tier-1 customer is a step change rather than a trend. Do not import file-format logic into a fungible bit.
- **Network effects do not exist here.** There is no mechanism by which one customer's use of a memory maker's bits makes those bits more valuable to another customer (see MOAT-P3). Scale in memory buys cost-per-bit rank and nothing else.
- **"Scale wins" and "recurring revenue is stable" are exactly the imports that break at the boundary** (see NARC-M2). Scale does not buy pricing power in a commodity, and in the down-leg it buys a larger fixed-cost base to absorb. Worse, the JV structures common in NAND make a maker's fixed costs *non-discretionary* — half the fixed cost is owed whether or not the wafers are taken. That is the inverse of the operating flexibility a quality screen assumes comes with scale.
- **"Cost leadership is a moat" transfers — and it is the ONLY moat that does.** This is the framework where cost-curve position is load-bearing rather than incidental (see MOAT-P4): where output is undifferentiated, the durable advantage is the ability to earn at the price that forces the marginal producer out. Note the inverse from the same rule cuts the other way in my software and footwear frameworks, where driving for lowest cost destroys the thing the customer is paying for. Do not carry that instinct in here.
- **The multiple direction is reversed.** In every other framework in this vault, a falling P/E on rising earnings is a signal to look harder. **Here it is the top.** This is VAL-T1 in its purest available form — the denominator, not the multiple — and the reason it belongs in the crossover section rather than only in the valuation one is that the *habit* of screening on a low forward multiple is what carries the damage across from the other frameworks. In memory, the multiple is the last thing to look at, not the first.
- **P/B is reversed too, in a way I have not seen elsewhere.** Everywhere else a stale book value understates a compounder. Here, at a peak, book value can more than double within four quarters, so a terrifying 13x trailing P/B and a benign 3.7x forward P/B are the same company on the same day. Anchor forward, and be explicit that the forward anchor is conditional on the peak year arriving.
- **High margins are not evidence of quality here** (see MOAT-T1). In software or branded consumer, an 80% gross margin is prima facie evidence of a franchise. In memory it is prima facie evidence of a shortage, and shortages end. Delete the margin from the argument and ask what is left.
- **The next-cohort lens does not apply** (see MOAT-M2). There is no trained cohort, no muscle memory, no brand formation clock. A memory customer re-decides at every qualification cycle. Nothing about the durability question here routes through cohort replacement — which means one of my more reliable diagnostic tools is simply silent, and silence must not be read as a clean bill.
- **"Multiple compression = opportunity" needs its cause named before it means anything** (see VAL-T2). In this industry, compression on cycle fear mean-reverts on a 6-8 quarter operating clock; compression on Chinese capacity, or on an architecture change that compresses the demand for bits themselves, need not. They look identical on a screen and the two candidates are both live as of 2026-07-30.
- **Drawdown depth is not a bottom signal and "quality on sale" is not a size argument** (see ASYM-T2). This industry has produced -81.7% and -98.2% drawdowns in living memory and a 21.5-year recovery on one of them. Size so that another leg down leaves you able to add, not needing to. In practical terms that means a smaller initial position in memory than the same conviction would justify in any other framework here.
- **A beat-and-raise met with a selloff means the debate is unresolved, and confirming operating data will not close it** (see NARC-P1). This industry produces the purest version of that state: record results, record margins and rising estimates met with a 30-56% complex-wide drawdown in five weeks. Do not read the record print as the resolution — name the observable that would actually close the debate (here: the sufficiency ratio through the 2027-28 capacity wave), and hold entry discipline until it prints.
- **The multiple is tracking consensus about a story, not the earnings themselves** (see NARC-M1). As of 2026-07-30, FY2027 consensus EPS for the purest NAND name had risen **86% in 90 days** with 6 upward revisions and zero downward in 30 days, while the stock halved. The market was voting against its own consensus. Neither side of that is "reality"; both are positions, and the multiple prices the second one.
- **Buyback logic inverts relative to my other frameworks** (see CAPA-P1). See "Capital allocation patterns" — the commodity dissent applies here, and the trough instrument is probably not the company's own stock.
- **Finally, do not import the assumption that the company matters as much as it does elsewhere.** In creative software or branded consumer, execution explains most of the dispersion. In memory, industry supply/demand explains most of it, and the company explains survival. Spend the research budget accordingly: on bits, capex and the cost curve, not on the CEO.

## What I've learned over time

Append-only log.

- 2026-07-30 (framework-bootstrap, v1 draft — no deep dive yet): **The DRAM/NAND divergence is the framework, and it was confirmed by a forecast published the same day this file was written.** TrendForce's 2026-07-30 note has DRAM staying tight and widening into 2027 while NAND supply growth outpaces demand with prices under downward pressure. If the AI demand shift were the *structural* fact, it would lift both. It does not — it lifts the one with the supply chokepoint. That is the cleanest available evidence that structure, not the AI story, is what is load-bearing, and it is why this file refuses to treat "memory" as one industry.
- 2026-07-30 (framework-bootstrap, v1 draft — honest gaps): this draft was built from secondary industry data plus primary SEC filings for one NAND name, and it carries four gaps the owner should close before it is used for a real decision. (1) **No competitor 10-K risk-factor read.** The skill's own research step asks for risk-factor language across 3-5 large players; the SK hynix US-listing prospectus, Micron's FY2025 10-K and the Kioxia filings are all unread, and the SK hynix prospectus in particular is the richest unabsorbed primary source in the industry. (2) **No through-cycle ROIC or segment-margin series comparing DRAM-weighted against NAND-only producers** — the central structural claim rests on a three-year, two-company net-income comparison. (3) **No cost-per-bit or absolute ASP series anywhere**, because no supplier discloses them; every KPI keyed to cost per bit currently needs a proxy. (4) **No dated spot-price series**, so the fastest leading indicator in the industry is not on this framework's monitoring surface at all.
- 2026-07-30 (framework-bootstrap, v1 draft — data-integrity finding worth generalising): a vendor data field that is *arithmetically impossible* is usually a period mismatch or a normalisation, not a business anomaly. The reported operating margin above gross margin for a NAND name resolved as a most-recent-quarter normalised operating margin quoted against a trailing-twelve-month gross margin — reconciling to four significant figures once the periods were separated, with no JV or one-off gain involved. Two independent researchers reached the same reconciliation. In the same pull, the vendor's enterprise value was understated by ~24% for all four names checked, and its annual "operating income" row differed from the filed GAAP line by more than $1.8bn in one year. **In this industry, reconcile the vendor data to the filing before building anything on it.**
- 2026-07-30 (SNDK deep-dive): **The EV/replacement-cost cross-check is unavailable for a JV participant, and its absence removes the only lens that has historically caught memory at a bottom.** This file prescribes replacement cost as secondary cross-check (a), on the reasoning that installed capacity has an unambiguous rebuild cost the market has repeatedly priced incumbents below at troughs. That reasoning silently assumes the company *owns* the capacity. SanDisk has **$649M of net PP&E and no fab**: its productive base is a 49.9% interest in equipment held by three JV entities inside buildings Kioxia owns, and it is contractually barred from building its own (10-Q q/e 2026-04-03 Note 10, verified). There is no rebuild cost to anchor to — only an obligation schedule ($6,543M of JV commitments, $993M of lease guarantees, $856M of prepaid building depreciation outstanding, $1.2bn owed to Kioxia over 2026-2029). **Before using the replacement-cost anchor on any memory name, check whether it owns fabs. Where it does not, say so and let the trough case rest on forward book alone rather than quietly dropping the cross-check.**
- 2026-07-30 (SNDK deep-dive): **The forward-book anchor should be run on TANGIBLE book, and this file's own worked example does not.** The P/B-against-forward-book rule above is the most useful idea here, but it was demonstrated on reported book value per share. SanDisk carries **$4,994M of goodwill — 36% of its $13,777M equity** — from Western Digital's 2016 acquisition, already impaired twice ($671M FY2023, $1,830M in the quarter ended 2025-03-28, verified). Goodwill on a commodity manufacturer that has written it down twice in three years is not book value that survives a trough, and a third impairment is a normal feature of the down-leg rather than a surprise. Stripping it moved SanDisk's post-FY2027 anchor from ~$329 to ~$296 per share — about 10% of the base-case fair value, all of it on the conservative side. Apply trough multiples to book *less goodwill*.
- 2026-07-30 (SNDK deep-dive): **Check where the company's fiscal year sits relative to the forecast crossover date before reading a consensus year as impossible.** TrendForce has NAND supply growth outpacing demand in 2027 with the balance turning positive in **2H27**; SanDisk's fiscal 2027 ends ~2027-07-02, so the consensus year the market is arguing about sits almost entirely *ahead* of the crossover. That does not make consensus right — momentum had already collapsed from +70-75% to +10-15% QoQ before the year began — but it does mean the bear case's strongest dated evidence lands in FY2028, not in the year being priced. In an industry where the sufficiency forecast is calendar-year and the issuers' fiscal years are not, this offset is worth computing explicitly every time rather than assuming the two line up.
- 2026-07-30 (SNDK deep-dive — what this dive could NOT do, so the next one does not assume it was done): **no earnings-call transcript was read for any of the last four quarters**, which is where the two questions that actually decide the NAND thesis live — how the multi-year customer agreements price, and what FY2027 capex intentions are. **No Kioxia filing was read either**, and for a Flash Ventures participant the partner's capex plan is a larger input to the supply question than anything the company itself discloses. Both gaps are carried into the conviction rather than netted out of it, and both should be closed before this framework is used on Kioxia, Micron's NAND segment, or SanDisk again.
- 2026-08-22 (SNDK deep-dive refresh): **The trough P/B series this framework carried did not survive primary verification, and the correction changes the floor.** Recomputed from EDGAR balance sheets against unadjusted price lows: Micron's cycle troughs are ~0.80-0.82x book (2016), ~1.03x (2019) and ~1.06-1.07x (2022-12) — the previously cited 0.97x and 1.32x reproduce from no trough price against any adjacent filing, and the "rising staircase" read was an artifact. SanDisk's own standalone trough (April 2025) was 0.44-0.47x reported and ~1.0x tangible book. The floor evidence is now: **memory bottoms near 1.0x book, with one 0.8x episode**; anything above ~1.1x used as a "trough" multiple is unsupported. A second lesson rides along: the vault's earlier "40.10" SNDK price point appears to have been back-solved from a claimed multiple rather than traded — when a figure and its multiple are cited together, verify the PRICE leg from the tape, not just the balance-sheet leg.
- 2026-08-22 (SNDK deep-dive refresh): **NAND now has contracted price floors at scale — and a two-tier disclosure of them.** SanDisk disclosed (2026-08-05) multi-year agreements covering >50% of FY2027 bits and ~2/3 of FY2028, $93.9bn of minimum revenue "at floor pricing", NBM gross margin "around 80%" (CFO, in Q&A — the calls carried more than the deck). But the audited 10-K never uses the words floor or ceiling, the deck's $16.5bn guarantee figure resolves to ~$7.7bn identifiable in the filing (of which $5.0bn is default-triggered collateral), and no renegotiation mechanics are disclosed. Two framework rules follow: (a) an LTA regime exists in NAND for the first time and any trough case must now be built floor-conditional — the pre-2026 trough analogues apply cleanly only if the floors fail; (b) **grade contract-regime claims by where they are disclosed** — deck-level characterization, call-level quantification and audited-filing language are three different evidence tiers, and the gaps between them are themselves findings.
