# Asymmetry, entry and exit

**Scope:** Given a value judgment, whether to act and how big — reward-to-risk thresholds for sizing, price-independent exit discipline, entry triggers and the anchors that go stale on them, whether a mispricing window is open or already closed, and how to read another investor's entries and exits as a pricing signal. Not how the value itself is derived (that is valuation discipline), and not the behavioural rules about logging, cadence and calibration (that is process discipline).

## Principles

### ASYM-P1 — Exit on thesis change, never on price

**ASYM-P1** — Exit only when the written thesis breaks: a price move, however large in either direction, is a reason to re-underwrite and never by itself a reason to sell. Every hold/trim/add table therefore carries a price-independent EXIT row keyed to the named kill switches in the thesis — and a worry for which no falsifiable switch can be written is not yet an exit case.

### ASYM-P2 — Size to the asymmetry, not to the franchise

**ASYM-P2** — Position size follows the reward-to-risk of the price in front of you, not the quality of the business: roughly 2:1 upside-to-downside is the threshold for a starting position, 3:1 or better is what justifies stepping conviction up, and roughly 1:1 is a PASS however good the franchise is (owner's own framework, 2026-05, unverified). Compute the ratio one way and say so: from the current price, upside to the **base-case** fair value over downside to the **bear-case** fair value, both read off the scenario table in that name's `valuation.md` rather than off a target band restated in the write-up, and neither discounted by its probability. Quote the bull case alongside; never blend it into the numerator. The probability-weighted value answers a different question — whether the price is fair — and is not this ratio's basis. The ratio inherits every error in the scenario table, so a stale or un-normalized range produces a confidently wrong size rather than no size.

## Mental models

### ASYM-M1 — The mispricing window opens on maximum bad news and closes fast

**ASYM-M1** — The best entries cluster at the moment of maximum *simultaneous* bad news, and the window shuts on the first sharp rally: after that move the easy compression-to-normal trade is gone and the remaining upside asks you to pay for resolutions that have not happened yet. Date-stamp when the window was open, at what price, and whether you acted — an undated window gets remembered as "I nearly bought it" instead of as calibration.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, UNH valuation.md "Read on current state" 2026-05-21 [unverified]: the generational-mispricing window is written up as having been open at the February-March 2026 low and closed by the time of the deep dive unless the price retreats again, with the multiple on the same forward earnings quantified at both prices (holds when: the bad news is cyclical or sentiment-driven; where the compression is structural or regulatory the "window" framing misleads badly — there the low is not a window but a waypoint, and buying maximum bad news is buying a business that is still deteriorating, see ASYM-T2)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, UNH thesis.md bear case #3 2026-05-21 [unverified]: the steelmanned bear case for a post-rally price is exactly that the price now requires trust on unresolved items, which is the same observation from the downside (holds when: the specific unresolved item is named — "requires trust" is a slogan unless you can say which resolution and by when)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — untested: the one window this vault has dated was dated retrospectively, after it had closed, with no decision taken while it was open. So the model has produced a label, not yet a trade or a documented miss with a price attached.

### ASYM-M2 — A disciplined investor's round trip is a pricing anchor

**ASYM-M2** — When a respected, slow-moving investor both enters and exits the same name inside a short window, treat the price zone they exited at as a dated observable — often a more useful anchor than any analyst target — and reconcile it against your own scenario range instead of reading a verdict off it. The behaviour supports at least three readings: the margin of safety closed at that price, diligence surfaced something the entry had not priced, or the capital simply found a better use. So any write-up that leans on the round trip must name which reading it is assuming and what evidence would separate that reading from the others; leaning on the anchor while naming none is borrowing someone else's conclusion without owning it.

## Traps

### ASYM-T1 — Stale anchor: waiting for a price the thesis has outgrown

**ASYM-T1** — Stale-anchor error: holding a fixed entry price while the fair value it was derived from drifts up, so the trigger recedes exactly as the thesis is being confirmed. When fundamentals confirm the bull case and the price runs away from the trigger, the required action is to re-underwrite the scenario range and reset the trigger — not to keep waiting on the old number, and not to chase the post-print move before that refresh is done.

### ASYM-T2 — Cheap can get cheaper, and stay there

**ASYM-T2** — A quality franchise can look cheap on the way down for years: compression from a peak rating to a trough one has run three to five years in the cases these frameworks record (owner's own frameworks, 2026-05, unverified). Never size to "this can't go lower" or "this can't stay cheap" — size so that another leg down leaves you able to add rather than needing to.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: validated]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, NKE thesis.md and ADBE thesis.md "Position sizing logic" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: two independent sizing sections written days apart both end on the same instruction — do not size to "this can't go lower" / "this can't stay cheap", it can — in one case against a cyclical-discount read and in the other against a disruption-narrative read (holds when: the franchise really is intact. Where the cheapness is structural erosion rather than cycle, holding a reserve to add into weakness is averaging into a melting business, and the thing that saves you there is the written kill switch, not the reserve)

## Open questions

- **What actually resets a stale anchor?** No rule says whether resetting an entry trigger needs a full deep-dive refresh or just a refreshed scenario range. The one live case has been sitting unrefreshed since 2026-07-17, which suggests the answer needs to be cheap enough to actually happen.
- **Do the three recorded conviction ratios get restated on the standardized basis?** ASYM-P2 now fixes the basis (base-over-bear off the `valuation.md` scenario table), and recomputing on it flatters none of the three existing conviction paragraphs. Restating those numbers is a thesis-body write and therefore owner-only, so until it happens the vault holds two ratios for each name — the one written at initiation and the one this rule computes.
- **How should permanent-loss bear cases enter the sizing ratio?** A reward-to-risk number treats a large drawdown and a total loss as the same kind of thing scaled differently, which is wrong for solvency, fraud and regulatory-destruction cases. Some cap, veto, or separate test is missing.
- **Does a pass need a time-based revisit as well as an observable one?** PROD-P6 requires an observable revisit condition, but a name that quietly re-rates over two years with no single observable firing never comes back into view.
