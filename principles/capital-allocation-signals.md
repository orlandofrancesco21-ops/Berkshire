# Capital allocation signals

**Scope:** Reading management by what it does with cash — repurchase timing and the multiple paid, capital return net of stock-based comp, M&A size and type discipline, behaviour after a blocked deal, insider open-market buying, and how to audit claimed capital discipline against full-cycle data instead of the IR deck. Not here: whether the business itself is durable (`business-quality-and-moats.md`), what the price implies (`valuation-discipline.md`), or whether to act on it (`asymmetry-entry-exit.md`).

## Principles

### CAPA-P1 — Judge a repurchase by the multiple, not the dollars

**CAPA-P1** — Judge a repurchase by the multiple it was executed at and by the share count actually retired, never by the dollars an IR deck reports as returned: at trough multiples repurchase is usually the highest-return use of a business's cash, and at peak multiples it destroys value while wearing the same label. Net it against stock-based-comp issuance before believing either read (see CAPA-M1).
`[evidence: contested | my record: validated]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md`, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` and `frameworks/us-managed-care-and-health-services.md` "Capital allocation patterns", 2026-05 [unverified]: three unrelated industries state the same law with local examples — trough repurchase is the highest-return use of capital, and levering up to buy near peak multiples can destroy as much value as the prior decade created (holds when: the trough is cyclical rather than existential, the balance sheet funds the buy without impairing the operating plan, and the franchise is intact — repurchasing into structural decline just makes a shrinking business shrink per share faster)
- **Voices:** **diverges** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md` "Capital allocation patterns", 2026-05-08 [unverified]: in commodity and capital-intensive businesses the counter-cyclical move at the trough is buying assets while they are cheap, funded by a balance sheet that was already repaired — deleveraging and variable dividends are top-of-cycle instruments there, done while prices are high precisely so the balance sheet is ready for the next trough. The trough job is staying seated and buying assets, not retiring shares, so the right trough instrument is often not the company's own stock. The same framework still red-flags repurchase at the top, so timing is not in dispute: the divergence is entirely about instrument, and it runs at both ends of the cycle — at the trough assets rather than stock, at the top distribution and debt paydown rather than repurchase (holds when: the trough price is set by a commodity management does not control, and contracted or hedged cash flow plus balance-sheet headroom are what fund survival through it; the divergence weakens where the trough is demand-driven and the balance sheet entered it unlevered, since then there is nothing to repair and own stock competes on price with third-party assets)
- **My record:** 2026-07-17 — `journal/2026-07-17-decision.md` ADBE entry names a trough-priced buyback authorization as one of three legs of an actual BUY, so this rule has directed real capital. The same audit graded a held name's near-total repurchase pause at its own trough as capital preservation rather than counter-cyclical buying, and no position has closed, so the rule has moved money without yet being settled by a realized outcome.

### CAPA-P2 — Credit discipline only against cycle data

**CAPA-P2** — Credit claimed capital discipline only against a full cycle of actual data — capex by year, M&A timing against asset and commodity price levels, repurchase timing against the multiple — because almost every management claims counter-cyclicality and few execute it. Where that history does not exist, the honest state is unknown, not clean.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md` "What makes a great business" trait 3 and bull archetype "Capital discipline is real this time", 2026-05-08 [unverified]: audit through the cycle data, not the IR deck — words are cheap and the latest budget at the latest peak is the test (holds when: at least one full peak-to-trough cycle exists under substantially the same management; for a new CEO, a recent IPO, or a post-restructuring balance sheet there is nothing to audit, and inferring discipline from stated policy is the error the rule exists to prevent)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — applied in every deep dive: the capital-allocation sections of the three company theses are five-year capex/buyback/dividend/M&A tables rather than management claims. Honest shortfall — five fiscal years is shorter than the full cycle this rule asks for, so the vault currently satisfies the method and not the span, and no decision has yet turned on a name failing this test.

### CAPA-P3 — Transformative M&A is guilty until proven

**CAPA-P3** — Put the burden of proof on the buyer for any acquisition above roughly a tenth of market cap (owner's own framework threshold, frameworks 2026-05, unverified): presume it destroys value until the integration case is made in operating terms, because the observed base rate on transformative deals is destruction, not because this deal looks bad. Bolt-ons at reasonable multiples that deepen an existing operating model are the only exception the vault has found to hold. Size is a screen, not the whole test — a smaller deal that imports liability can be worse.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, "Capital allocation patterns" in `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md`, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` and `frameworks/us-managed-care-and-health-services.md`, 2026-05 [unverified]: three unrelated industries state the base rate and the exception route. The strongest form is the apparel framework's — "M&A is almost universally destructive", with "default to skepticism on any deal beyond a small bolt-on" — while managed care records that transformative deals destroyed value more often than they created it and that every exception named was small relative to market cap and *required deep operational integration*, which is what makes an operating-terms integration case the way out. The size threshold itself comes from the software framework, whose own verb at that line is scrutiny rather than presumption; the presumption is carried by the base rate, not by the threshold (holds when: the acquirer is buying revenue, category adjacency, or a competitor; the rule inverts for a buyer whose operating model is itself the integration engine, where serial bolt-ons are the business)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, `sectors/us-managed-care-and-health-services/companies/UNH/thesis.md` "Capital allocation track record", 2026-05-21 [unverified]: the era's single biggest allocation mistake was a deal well inside the size threshold, because what it imported — remediation cost, antitrust scrutiny, ongoing legal liability — scaled independently of the price paid (holds when: the target carries regulated data, licences, or contingent liability; then the test is liability transferred, not percentage of market cap)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — pre-committed but never fired: one held name carries a written kill switch keyed to an M&A announcement above a tenth of market cap (ADBE thesis, 2026-05-20), and no evaluated name has announced a deal since. The vault has graded past deals, not lived through one.

## Mental models

### CAPA-M1 — Capital return is net of stock-based comp

**CAPA-M1** — Net capital return is gross repurchases minus stock-based-comp issuance, and the confirmation is diluted share count, not the buyback headline: a company repurchasing heavily while share count stays flat has returned that cash to employees, not to owners.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Red flags" (SBC-exceeding-buybacks bullet) and "Capital allocation patterns" (net-of-SBC bullet), 2026-05-20 [unverified]: software is the highest-SBC industry, the gross buyback number minus SBC issuance is the true capital return, and many names fail this test by more than the IR deck implies (holds when: SBC is a structural component of pay — in low-SBC industries the adjustment changes no decision; and the argument is about the level of SBC, not its existence, since equity comp tied to per-share outcomes is a real cost that buys real alignment)
- **My record:** 2026-05-20 — the software deep dive made the adjustment and cleared the name: SBC as a share of revenue was high but the repurchase program was still clearly net negative to share count (ADBE thesis "Capital allocation track record"). Adjustment performed, no decision has yet turned on it, and no name has been rejected on this test.

### CAPA-M2 — Read the allocator through buyback price discrimination

**CAPA-M2** — Read the allocator, not the transaction: whether repurchase velocity moves inversely to the multiple across a full cycle is the cleanest single observable of allocator quality, and the inverse pattern — aggressive at the peak, paused at the trough, dividend cut at the bottom — is the tell of a poor one. CAPA-P1 values the repurchase; this grades the person doing it.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: mixed]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `sectors/creative-and-design-software/companies/ADBE/thesis.md` "Capital allocation track record", 2026-05-20 [unverified]: the judgment that mattered was not buyback size but that velocity was *not* raised at the multiple peak and *was* raised at the trough, with the largest authorization in company history announced at the cheapest multiple of its modern era (holds when: a full peak-to-trough multiple cycle is observable under substantially the same management, and the authorization is being executed rather than merely announced — an authorization is permission, not a purchase)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, `sectors/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel/companies/NKE/thesis.md` Update log 2026-07-17, 2026-07-17 [unverified]: a near-total repurchase pause at the trough was graded capital preservation rather than poor allocation, because the company's own turnaround — not merely its cycle — was unresolved (holds when: the trough is company-specific and the operating outcome is genuinely open; grade preservation neutral and keep grading, but a second cycle of preservation at cheap prices is a verdict, not prudence)
- **My record:** 2026-07-17 — mixed by design: the test produced one clean positive read (software holding, no acceleration at the peak and maximum authorization at the trough) and one it could not resolve (footwear holding, paused at the trough with the pause defensible under turnaround uncertainty). The observable separates good allocators from bad ones and does not separate cautious ones from bad ones.

### CAPA-M3 — Grade the insider cluster, don't just note it

**CAPA-M3** — Treat CEO-plus-multiple-director open-market purchases at depressed prices as corroboration for a pessimism-overdone read, and grade the cluster — whose money, at what price relative to the insiders' own prior basis, scheduled or unscheduled — rather than recording that insiders bought.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `sectors/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel/companies/NKE/thesis.md` Update log 2026-07-17 (bull-case grade 2 and "Unanticipated disclosures"), 2026-07-17 [unverified]: a CEO buying twice on the open market as the price fell, with two directors alongside, was logged as a governance signal consistent with the pessimism-overdone read and as something the thesis had not contemplated (holds when: purchases are open-market, unscheduled, and material against the insider's own compensation — scheduled 10b5-1 buys, option exercises and company-funded purchases carry no signal; and the cluster is corroboration only, never a thesis leg, since insiders are as capable of being wrong about their own turnaround as anyone else)
- **My record:** 2026-07-17 — logged as supportive colour and explicitly graded not print-gradeable; no entry, sizing, or conviction change has moved on an insider cluster. Sourcing caveat carried forward: the observation came from press coverage, so it caps at unverified until the filings themselves are read.

## Traps

### CAPA-T1 — The consolation deal after a blocked one

**CAPA-T1** — After a blocked or abandoned acquisition, management's need to "do something" produces a worse deal than the one it lost: treat roughly the following year and a half (owner's own framework, 2026-05, unverified) as a window in which any new acquisition carries a higher burden of proof, and read building the capability internally instead as a positive quality signal.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Red flags" (failed-M&A bullet) and `sectors/creative-and-design-software/companies/ADBE/thesis.md` "Capital allocation track record", 2026-05-20 [unverified]: the canonical observed case paid a large termination fee for nothing, then did not chase a lower-quality target, did not pivot strategy in panic, and built the capability internally — which the deep dive scored as discipline rather than as recovery (holds when: the deal was stopped by a regulator, a court, or the target; a company that walks away on price is demonstrating the very discipline this trap warns about, so no window opens)
- **My record:** 2026-05-20 — the software deep dive graded a real blocked-deal response under this test and scored it disciplined, which fed the conviction rating behind a live position; but the trap itself has never fired in a name the vault holds, so nothing has been paid for it.

## Open questions

- Is the energy divergence on CAPA-P1 a scope limit or a separate rule? Two readings survive the vault's own text: either trough repurchase is right everywhere except where trough cash flow funds survival, or commodity businesses run an entirely different playbook (assets at the trough; variable dividends and debt paydown at the peak). Resolving it needs a primary capital-cycle source — the vault cites a Marathon-style capital-cycle framework second-hand in `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md` and has absorbed nothing.
- How long does a paused trough buyback stay defensible under CAPA-M2 before it becomes a verdict on the allocator? The vault has one live case and no threshold, and grading it by feel is exactly what the item was written to prevent.
- Does insider cluster buying (CAPA-M3) carry signal net of base rates, or is it a story that reads well in drawdowns? One observation, press-sourced, no base rate.
- No external investor voice on capital allocation exists in this vault yet. Shareholder letters from long-record allocators are tier-1 for this theme under "Maturity and tier-1 sources" in `docs/build-plan.md` and would be the highest-value ingestion for this file — every item here currently rests on the owner's own frameworks.
