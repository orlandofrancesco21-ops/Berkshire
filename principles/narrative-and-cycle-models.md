# Narrative and cycle models

**Scope:** How stories, consensus and cycles move a price independently of the fundamentals — the fundamentals-confirming/narrative-unresolved state, what a multiple is actually tracking, the operating series that turn before the income statement, capital-cycle timing (still an open question here rather than a stored model — see Open questions), the recurring bull-case archetypes that make buyers overpay, and the assumptions that stop working when carried from one industry into another. Not how to convert a price into a value (valuation discipline), not whether the payoff justifies acting (asymmetry, entry and exit), not whether a moat is real on its merits (business quality and moats). This file answers *why the market believes what it believes, and what would have to happen for the belief to turn.*

## Principles

### NARC-P1 — Fundamentals confirming, narrative unresolved

**NARC-P1** — When a beat-and-raise print is met with a selloff to new lows, the narrative is unresolved and confirming operating data does not resolve it; hold entry and sizing discipline unchanged until you can name the specific observable that would close the debate, because the multiple can discount faster than per-share value compounds.

### NARC-P2 — Name the industry's leading series, then watch that

**NARC-P2** — Every industry has an operating series that turns two to four quarters before revenue does (owner's own frameworks, 2026-05, unverified); name that series for the industry in front of you and monitor it instead of the income statement, because by the time the P&L shows the turn the multiple has already moved.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` KPI table and Red flags 2026-05-20 [unverified]: inventory growing ahead of revenue for two or more quarters flags markdowns and gross-margin compression before earnings do (holds when: the business builds physical inventory against a forecast, so a demand miss lands on the balance sheet first)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/us-managed-care-and-health-services.md` "Red flags" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: persistently unfavourable prior-period development is the most reliable leading indicator of medical-loss pressure two to four quarters out (holds when: the accounting depends on management's estimate of unreported costs, so the reserve line carries the news before the loss ratio does)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" and "Red flags" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: net dollar retention declines are leading indicators, not lagging ones — the cohort turns while contracted revenue is still rolling forward (holds when: revenue renews on a lag, which is exactly what delays the P&L signal)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Industry-specific KPIs" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: several of the best leading series are disclosed voluntarily "where available", so a trigger keyed to one dies the quarter the company stops publishing it — every such trigger needs a named fallback proxy (holds when: the observable is not a required line in the statements)

## Mental models

### NARC-M1 — The multiple tracks consensus, not reality

**NARC-M1** — A multiple prices what the consensus currently believes about a strategy, not whether the strategy works; the verdict on a major strategic pivot is rarely available inside a two-to-three-year window, so treat "X is the future" framings as unpriced opinion while the cycle is still running.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` "What I've learned over time" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: a branded-consumer distribution pivot ran about seven years from "brilliant strategic move" to "obvious mistake", and the consensus took most of those years to break (owner's own framework write-back, 2026-05-20, unverified) (holds when: the pivot's payoff shows up in customer behaviour across several product cycles rather than in a metric that reports next quarter)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Key drivers of returns" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: multiples expand when the market believes the incumbent is on the right side of the next architecture shift and compress violently when it does not — the belief moves first and the numbers arbitrate later (holds when: the shift is legible as a story before it is legible in the financials)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Common bull-case archetypes" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: the model cuts both ways — an unformed consensus is also the source of the opportunity, so this is a reason to discount the *verdict*, not a reason to avoid companies mid-pivot (holds when: the price already assumes the pivot fails, so you are being paid for the ambiguity rather than paying for it)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — untested: the seven-year case is historical, reconstructed during a deep dive rather than lived through a position. No entry or exit in this vault has yet been timed against a pivot verdict.

### NARC-M2 — Imported assumptions are narratives, not laws

**NARC-M2** — An assumption carried in from the last industry you studied — scale wins, recurring revenue is stable, the sticky customer stays sticky — is a claim about a mechanism, and when you cannot name the mechanism that produces it in the industry in front of you, you are importing a story rather than establishing a fact.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: scale buys endorsement budgets and supply-chain terms but does not defend brand heat; there is no recurring revenue at all in a one-time discretionary purchase; and the wholesale partner who looks like a sticky enterprise customer re-allocates shelf space every quarter to whoever has the heat (holds when: demand is driven by cultural preference, which respects neither size nor installed base)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/creative-and-design-software.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: architecture shifts redistribute the category, so scale in the old architecture can be a millstone rather than an advantage, and subscription revenue decays through such shifts rather than holding bond-like (holds when: an architecture change is live — within one architecture the same assumptions mostly do hold)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/us-managed-care-and-health-services.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: regulatory exposure scales with scale, sometimes faster than the cost advantage does; and annually re-priced premium is not bond-like — one bad pricing cycle can compress earnings by roughly a third (`frameworks/us-managed-care-and-health-services.md`, 2026-05-20, unverified) (holds when: price resets on a contractual clock, so "recurring" describes the contract rather than the economics)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-08 [unverified]: in commodity sub-sectors the lowest-cost producer earns the cycle and scale becomes a liability when it rolls (more fixed cost to amortize); revenue can be recurring while the spread behind it is not, so the real test is the contracted or regulated share of *cash flow*, not of revenue (holds when: the output is a commodity, which severs the link between size and pricing)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, the four framework files' "Mental-model crossover risks" sections 2026-05 [unverified]: the assumptions are usually correct inside the industry that produced them — the failure is at the boundary, so the test fires on crossing industries, not every time the word "scale" appears in a thesis (holds when: you can say which framework the assumption came from; if you cannot, it is model memory, not analysis)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — untested: all four framework crossover sections were written in the same 2026-05 build, before any position was sized against them, so no decision has yet been changed by the check.

## Traps

### NARC-T1 — Buying an archetype instead of a business

**NARC-T1** — The stories that make you overpay are a short recurring list — the brand is forever, capital discipline is real this time, the moat is too deep to disrupt, the region is a vast untapped opportunity — and the trap is not that the thesis restates one, it is buying it without naming what killed that same story last cycle.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, the four framework files' "Common bull-case archetypes" sections 2026-05 [unverified]: each framework maintains its industry's archetype list precisely so a live bull case can be matched against it before capital moves (holds when: the archetype is doing load-bearing work in the thesis rather than appearing as colour)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md` bull archetype "Capital discipline is real this time" 2026-05-08 [unverified]: words are cheap and the latest budget is the test — an archetype is disarmed by an observable, never by management's restatement of it (holds when: the claim is about future behaviour, which is exactly when it is unfalsifiable at the time of purchase)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, the four framework files' "Common bull-case archetypes" sections 2026-05 [unverified]: archetypes recur because they are sometimes right, so matching one is not a reason to pass — the test is whether the specific disconfirming observable that broke it last cycle is being monitored (holds when: last cycle's break is documented well enough to name the observable; where it is not, treat the archetype as unpriced)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — untested: the archetype lists exist in all four frameworks but no thesis in this vault yet records having matched its bull case against them, and the vault has no closed positions to score the trap against.

### NARC-T2 — The operator fighting the last war

**NARC-T2** — Operators who built their careers on one cycle's thesis double down on it when that cycle turns; when the strategy under question *is* the executive's personal signature, discount their read of the turn and weigh what a successor changes over what management says.
`[evidence: emerging | my record: untested]` (as-of 2026-07-28)

- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` bear archetype "The CEO is fighting the last war" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: operators who built their resumes in one cycle — direct-to-consumer, a single growth geography, athleisure — often double down on those theses when the cycle turns (holds when: reversing the strategy would be a public admission about the operator's own record, so the incentive runs against the evidence)
- **Voices:** **agrees** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: the transferable signal is tenure plus cultural fluency in *this* industry, not general operating pedigree — an operator imported from a different industry's playbook is the higher-risk case (holds when: the industry's success drivers are tacit rather than analytical)
- **Voices:** **qualifies** — Owner's own analysis, `frameworks/branded-athletic-footwear-and-apparel.md` "Mental-model crossover risks" 2026-05-20 [unverified]: the mirror-image error is real — a successor with no stake in the old thesis can over-correct and dismantle an asset that was working, so watch what is deliberately *kept* as closely as what is reversed (holds when: the previous strategy was partly right, which is the usual case for a strategy that lasted long enough to become a signature)
- **My record:** 2026-07-28 — untested prospectively: the vault's observed case is retrospective (NKE thesis.md "Management quality", 2026-05-20, records a technology operator imported into a brand business), and by the time that thesis was written the operator had already been replaced — so the trap has never been used to judge a sitting executive.

## Open questions

- **What actually resolves an unresolved narrative, and how long does it take?** NARC-P1 says confirming fundamentals do not close a structural debate, but the vault has no case where a debate *did* close — so there is no observed resolution tell and no base rate for how long the state persists. First closed position under a live disruption narrative should be written back here.
- **Is there a leading indicator for the narrative itself,** as distinct from the leading indicators of fundamentals in NARC-P2? Sell-side language, short interest, options skew and coverage tone are all candidates and none of them is in this vault. Until one is, "the narrative turned" can only be observed after the fact.
- **No external investor voice on cycles or sentiment exists here yet.** The only named outside sources in the whole vault are a capital-cycle framework cited second-hand and a disruption theory cited in order to reject it — both capped at unverified, and after the 2026-07-28 retirements below neither is carried as a Voices line in this file any more (the disruption relay now sits in MOAT-M1 and VAL-T2, the capital-cycle relay in MOAT-P4). Every Voices line here is the owner's own analysis, which means "what do big investors say about this kind of situation" currently cannot be answered. This is the highest-value ingestion gap in the file.
- **Does the capital cycle survive translation to capital-light industries?** *(This is the question the retired M4 ordinal below existed to answer, kept open here.)* The energy framework's own crossover note says the lag length is the whole model and it is industry-specific: where "capacity" is engineering headcount, model-training spend or brand investment rather than plant, the build time collapses and the cycle stops being readable this way. Whether an analogue holds in terms of *irreversible multi-year commitment* rather than physical capacity is genuinely unknown, and it matters, because most of the vault's coverage is capital-light. Resolution needs a second, non-energy industry instance of committed capacity marking the top — the trigger to re-mint this as a model, at the next free ordinal.
- **Retired ordinals in this file (2026-07-28), so a missing number is not read as a lost item.** IDs are never renumbered or reused, so both gaps are permanent and the next mental model here takes the M5 slot. (a) The **M3** ordinal held "erosion shows up in new adopters, not the installed base"; it duplicated MOAT-M2 off the same two locators and graded the same two 2026-07-17 debriefs incompatibly (`untested` here against `mixed` there). The rule now lives only in MOAT-M2, which is the right owner because the claim is about franchise durability and carries the terminal-value question this file's version lacked; its proxy-disclosure caveat was folded in there. (b) The **M4** ordinal held the capital cycle. Every voice traced to `frameworks/energy-and-cleantech.md`, which fails the routing test in `principles/README.md` ("true of one industry" routes to `frameworks/`), and no second-industry instance could be found — the nearest candidate, the creative-software hype-cycle trait, is about a company's own capital allocation at peak multiples, not about aggregate committed capacity, and routes to capital-allocation signals. The content stays where it already lives, in that framework's "Key drivers of returns" bullet and its "Capital cycle turning" bear archetype, and the cross-industry question is parked as an Open question above per the README's "park it until a second and third instance show the theme is real".
- **Boundary to watch:** this file and business-quality-and-moats both touch moat durability. The 2026-07-28 audit resolved two live collisions on that line by moving the network-effects test to MOAT-P3 and the cohort-replacement mechanism to MOAT-M2, leaving NARC-M2 with the boundary-crossing failure alone. The standing rule that produced those moves: a defect in the moat routes to MOAT; an imported *belief* about a mechanism routes here. Watch for the next candidate that reads as both, and route it once rather than storing it twice.
