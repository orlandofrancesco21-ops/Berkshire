---
name: fund-deep-dive
description: Produce a full long-form analysis of a single fund (ETF, index fund, or other listed wrapper) as an ALLOCATION decision rather than a buy/pass on a business. Grounded in two lenses — the universal vehicle framework (frameworks/etf-vehicle-mechanics.md) and the per-asset-class exposure framework (frameworks/{exposure-slug}.md) — and assumes both already exist. Output is thesis.md, valuation.md and the one-page ic-sheet.md in funds/{TICKER}/, where the valuation is an expected REAL RETURN range over a stated horizon, not a price. Also handles the refresh of an existing fund thesis. Triggered by "deep dive on {ETF}", "fund-deep-dive X", "should I hold X or Y for the {exposure} sleeve", "refresh the X fund thesis", or "how much should I allocate to X".
---

# fund-deep-dive

## When to use

The user is deciding how much of the portfolio to put into a fund, and against what alternatives. Common phrasings:

- "Deep dive on [ETF ticker or ISIN]"
- "Should I own [fund A] or [fund B] for the [exposure] sleeve?"
- "How much [exposure] should I hold, and through what?"
- "Refresh the [fund] thesis — the annual report is out"

Not this skill: a single company inside a fund (`company-deep-dive`), or ranking three funds already deep-dived (that is a compare pass, and its precondition is three fund documents in the same exposure).

## Inputs

- The fund's **ISIN**, or enough to resolve one (name + listing + currency). A bare ticker is not an input, it is an ambiguity — see step 1.
- The **exposure slug** the fund belongs to (must already have a framework; if not, stop and suggest `framework-bootstrap` for that exposure first, built from `frameworks/_template-exposure.md` — an asset class is not an industry and the industry template asks the wrong questions of it).
- The **horizon** the owner intends to hold it for, and the **allocation question** it is competing in (new sleeve, replacement for an existing holding, or deployment of cash). If either is missing, ask — the same fund is a different decision at 3 years and at 20, and an allocation with no named alternative is a rating, not a decision.

## Process

0. **Determine mode: fresh or REFRESH.** If `funds/{TICKER}/thesis.md` already exists, this run is a **REFRESH**. In refresh mode:
   - Read the existing `thesis.md` and `valuation.md` in full before pulling any new data. The old expected-return decomposition is calibration data: the point of writing each term separately was so a later run could grade which term was wrong.
   - **Preserve the Update log verbatim.** Every prior dated entry stays exactly as written — the first entry is the original allocation case.
   - Rewrite the body sections in place (what the exposure is, case for, case against, vehicle assessment, kill switches, Principles check, conviction, allocation logic) to reflect current reality. The Principles check is re-run from step 3b, never carried over.
   - Append `### YYYY-MM-DD — deep-dive refresh` to the Update log: which term of the decomposition moved and why, what the realized tracking difference and ongoing charge actually came in at versus what the last run assumed, any kill-switch rewording, and where the allocation lands now.
   - Apply the same rules to `valuation.md`: rewrite the decomposition table and the allocation bands in place, preserve and append to its update history.
   - **Regenerate `ic-sheet.md` whole** (step 8). It is a derived view with no log; a refresh that leaves a stale sheet standing produces two verdicts on disk, and the sheet is the one a hurried reader will believe.
   - The refresh trigger for a fund is not an earnings date. "Stale thesis (canonical)" clause (b) maps here to **the fund's annual report** — that is the fund's print, and it is the only document that says what the wrapper actually cost. Index methodology changes, a TER change, a share-class merger or closure notice, and a change of index all count as material events on the same footing.

   Fresh mode proceeds from the fund variants of the templates as described below.

1. **Identify the fund unambiguously.** Per "Funds tier (canonical)", the ISIN is the identity and the ticker is not — the same fund lists on several exchanges under different tickers and currencies, and a distributing and an accumulating share class are different objects. Getting this wrong means analysing a document set that does not describe what will be bought. Fill **every** row of the Identity table in `funds/_TICKER/thesis.md` before analysing anything, plus the three fields that table leaves to judgment and that the rest of this process depends on:
   - **The index's return variant** — price / net total return / gross total return. "MSCI World" is not an index specification, and the variant is what every tracking-difference figure in step 4 is measured against.
   - **Replication method** — full physical, sampled physical, or synthetic, and for synthetic the counterparty structure. It drives both the cost analysis and the tax analysis.
   - **Hedged or unhedged**, and the fund's own base currency, which is usually neither the trading currency nor the owner's — and is not a source of return.

   Search the existing `funds/` folders for the ISIN before creating a new one. Where two share classes are both genuine candidates, the document chooses between them explicitly in step 6; never analyse one and leave the other implied, which is the exact failure the ISIN discipline exists to prevent.

2. **Load both lenses, in this order** — the two-lens contract is set by "Funds tier (canonical)". Read `frameworks/etf-vehicle-mechanics.md` first (universal: it applies whatever the fund holds), then `frameworks/{exposure-slug}.md` for the asset class. Read them **before** touching the data: the vehicle framework decides which cost questions are worth the tokens, and the exposure framework decides how the expected-return range in step 5 is built. Do not apply a generic ETF checklist from memory; that is what these two files exist to replace.
   - The umbrella rule from `company-deep-dive` step 1b carries over unchanged: a framework marked `**Umbrella framework:** yes` is triage-only. A broad exposure framework with a sub-exposure navigator does not license a decision-grade read of the sub-exposure — bootstrap the sub-exposure framework, or say the analysis is triage.
   - If either lens is missing and nothing is concurrently producing it, stop and name which one. If this run was orchestrated alongside a lens being written in parallel, per "Concurrent orchestration (canonical)" re-check rather than stopping at the first miss, read the lens **fresh** when it lands, and record in the Update log that it arrived mid-run.

3. **Pull the data — and say which document is authoritative for which figure.** This is the step where fund analysis usually goes wrong, because the most available numbers are the least authoritative ones. The mapping is not negotiable:

   | Figure | Authoritative source | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | What the exposure actually is | the **index methodology document** | inclusion, weighting, capping, buffers, rebalance schedule, the return variant. The rulebook is the mandate; the product name is marketing |
   | Actual costs incurred, securities-lending revenue and its split, transaction costs, swap costs, withholding tax suffered, realized tracking difference | the **fund's own annual report** (audited) | the only document that says what the wrapper cost rather than what it advertises |
   | Stated ongoing charge, risk indicator, cost illustration, the fund's own tax-relevant characterisation | **KIID/KID and prospectus** | a stated figure, not a realized one |
   | Composition, concentration, overlap with what is already owned | the **full holdings file** | the top-10 table on a factsheet cannot answer an overlap question |
   | NAV and price history, premium/discount, spread, volume | exchange and provider data | every figure stamped per "Price and data sourcing (canonical)" |
   | Trailing performance | the factsheet, and it is the **weakest** input in the file | see Notes |

   Prefer the primary over any aggregator: never take an ongoing charge, a tracking difference, or a distribution figure from a screener when the annual report exists, and label anything that could not be traced to a primary per "Verification labels (canonical)". Annual reports are PDFs — extract with `scripts/pdf-extract.py` and honour the extractor lesson in "Source archive and deep retrieval (canonical)"; a near-empty extraction is a tooling failure, not an empty document.

   For Claude Code: parallelize this. One subagent per source — annual report, KID/prospectus, index methodology, holdings, price/NAV history, peer funds on the same exposure. Give each one the ISIN and the share class, not the ticker.

3b. **Load the principles lens.** Read the Index in `principles/README.md`, then open the files whose `**Scope:**` line bears on this situation — the Scope line, not the index title. Note the items, by permanent ID per "Permanent IDs and addressing (canonical)", that engage the facts of *this allocation* rather than the ones that sound adjacent to funds in general. Test staleness before relying on an item per "Stale principle (canonical)" and carry `[stale: {which condition}]` on the ID rather than citing it silently.

   Expect a thinner harvest than a company deep-dive, and do not compensate — "Funds tier (canonical)" sets out which themes usually bear and which do not, and notes that allocation-specific principles are deliberately unseeded. So the item that would most help an allocation decision often does not exist yet; the right response is the sentinel now and a candidate for step 9b later, never a stretched citation. Per "Principle authority (canonical)" this lens is advisory and stays advisory: it produces the checklist section in step 6 and a line the conviction paragraph has to answer, never the verdict in step 10 and never a kill switch.

4. **Assess the vehicle** against every item in the vehicle framework's checklist. The framework owns the list; this step owns the verdict, and the verdict has three parts:
   - **A one-line vehicle verdict:** SOUND / USABLE WITH CAVEATS / BROKEN FOR THIS PURPOSE. "For this purpose" is load-bearing — a wrapper that is wrong for a 20-year hold can be right for a two-year one, and the horizon from Inputs decides which cost dominates.
   - **The all-in annual drag, as a range, built from separately-sourced terms:** ongoing charge; the gap between that charge and the **realized multi-year tracking difference** against the index in its correct return variant (comparing an accumulating NAV to a price index manufactures either large alpha or large drag, and both are artifacts); index turnover and rebalance costs from the methodology; swap spread if synthetic; securities-lending revenue credited back to the fund, stated net of the counterparty and collateral risk it buys; and the trading spread plus brokerage **amortized over the intended holding period**. Each term carries source, date and a verification label. Where a term genuinely cannot be established — an undisclosed lending split, an annual report that predates a fee change — widen the range and say so. A wide honest range beats a narrow invented one.
   - **The peer table.** A fund's peer set is other funds giving the same exposure, never the companies inside it. At least two alternatives, compared on: ISIN, index and variant, replication, domicile, ongoing charge, realized tracking difference, fund size, distribution policy, spread, and the tax characterisation from step 7. If a cleaner or cheaper clone exists, that finding **is** the result of this run, and step 10 routes it.
   - Also check survival: share-class size, provider history of closing or merging sub-scale funds, and what a forced closure would cost the owner. That cost is a tax event, not an inconvenience, and it belongs in the vehicle kill switches in step 6.

5. **Assess the exposure and form the expected-return range**, using the method the exposure framework prescribes. Build it as a decomposition where each term is separately falsifiable and separately sourced, because a single expected-return number cannot be graded later and this one will be:
   - **Starting yield** — the income the exposure actually pays today, net of withholding actually suffered at fund level, stamped with its date.
   - **Real growth** of the underlying per-unit cash flows — not GDP, not index price history. State the base period and why it is representative.
   - **Valuation change** — where the exposure's valuation sits in its own history (a percentile, with the series named and dated), and what the scenario assumes about reversion, over what horizon. VAL-T2 applies here exactly as it does to a single name: a valuation percentile above history can be composition change rather than expensiveness, and the two are indistinguishable on the chart. Say which one you are asserting, and what would show you were wrong.
   - **Costs** — from step 4, carried in as a line, not folded into a fudge.
   - **Tax** — from the step-7 check, carried in with its confidence label.
   - **Currency** — its own line, never blended into expected return, and never silently assumed to net to zero either. State what the scenario assumes about the base-currency effect and on what basis; if the answer is that no defensible expectation exists, write that and let the term carry a range. For a hedged share class the hedge cost is a real, quantifiable annual number and belongs in the drag, not here. Whatever the expectation, the currency term does most of its work in the drawdown distribution, which is step 6's allocation logic rather than this table.

   Move the causally linked terms together across bear/base/bull per VAL-P4 — high growth paired with further multiple expansion is one coherent scenario; pairing bear growth with bull multiple manufactures a spread narrower than reality. The output is a range per VAL-P1, over the horizon named in Inputs, restated in the same breath as every number.

   Run `python scripts/exposure-history.py {TICKER}` for the realized distribution, drawdown depth and time-to-recovery, and read its independent-window count rather than its averages. It cannot answer the valuation-conditional question — what followed *from this starting percentile* — and that gap is filled by an absorbed source with a locator, never from memory. If no such source is in `library/`, say so, queue it in `sources.md`, and let the step-7 historical section carry the hole explicitly.

6. **Write `funds/{TICKER}/thesis.md`** using the template in `funds/_TICKER/thesis.md`, which carries the section set and the per-section prompts. What this step owns beyond the template:
   - **What this fund actually holds** is written from the index rulebook, not the product name. Where the label and the holdings disagree, the holdings win — "Evidence weighting (canonical)" applied to a wrapper: the marketing sheet is commentary, the methodology and the holdings file are the record.
   - **The case against** must include why this product exists and who is selling it. A thematic wrapper launched after the theme's run is a distribution decision by the provider, and it belongs in the bear case as such.
   - **Vehicle assessment** carries step 4 whole: the verdict, the drag range, the peer table, the named best alternative. It replaces the company thesis's capital-allocation and management sections, and the file says so in a line — a future reader has to see that they were substituted rather than dropped.
   - **Kill switches** split exposure-level from vehicle-level per "Funds tier (canonical)". PROD-P3 applies unchanged: threshold, timeframe, and an observable the provider or index sponsor actually publishes. Add the closure and merger case from step 4 to the vehicle side — a forced closure is a taxable event, not an inconvenience.
   - **Principles check** — same contract and the same lint gate as a company thesis, and the fund tier is inside it. Expect the sentinel to be right more often here (step 3b).
   - **Conviction states two verdicts in two sentences** — the exposure case, and the vehicle's fitness. Blended into one number, neither can be graded later, and the common finding of this skill is precisely that they disagree. It must answer every VIOLATES line: answer, not obey.
   - **Allocation logic** is the output of the whole run. Compute the overlap with what is already held from the holdings file rather than asserting it from names, and write the assumed drawdown in as a number (see Notes).
   - **Update log** headings are `### YYYY-MM-DD — initial thesis` and `### YYYY-MM-DD — deep-dive refresh`, verbatim. The Principles-check and IC-sheet gates key on those exact strings, so a thesis that invents its own heading switches both gates off while the lint prints `[OK]` — this vault's signature failure, which is why the strings are specified rather than left to taste.

7. **Write `funds/{TICKER}/valuation.md`** using the template in `funds/_TICKER/valuation.md`. It is canonical for this fund's return scenarios per VAL-P5, and per "Funds tier (canonical)" its output is an expected real return range over a stated horizon, with allocation bands rather than price bands. What this step owns beyond the template:
   - The step-5 terms go in as **rows that stay separable** — each cell traceable to its own source with its own verification label. A term that had to be collapsed into another is a term nobody can grade next year.
   - **The vehicle drag row carries its own confidence label** and should look different from every other row, because it is the only nearly-deterministic one.
   - **The historical section states the sample's limits** in the same breath as its numbers: overlapping windows are not independent observations, and a long series spans regimes the index composition no longer resembles.
   - **VAL-P3's independence test applies to the lenses in this file.** The decomposition and the realized-distribution read are only a cross-check if they interrogate different inputs; three views computed off one valuation series are one lens counted three times. Where they diverge, name the conservative one rather than averaging.
   - **The EXIT row names which kill switch fires it** — exposure-level means leaving the asset class, vehicle-level means switching funds, and per ASYM-P1 neither is ever triggered by the quote alone. `valuation-check` reads this table, so write it to be read mechanically.

   **The tax check (German resident, EUR base) is a return driver, not an appendix.** Encode what to check and why it matters, label confidence honestly, and state plainly that the specifics need his tax advisor's confirmation. At minimum: the domicile's treaty position and the withholding actually leaked at fund level (an index's net-total-return variant already embeds a withholding assumption that may not match this fund's); the partial-exemption regime for equity funds and the asset-ratio threshold a fund must keep to qualify; the advance-lump-sum charge on accumulating wrappers and the fact that it is owed in cash in years with no distribution; the timing difference between distributing and accumulating share classes, which is not the same question as the amount; the treatment of synthetic replication of US-dividend exposure; and the allowance and flat-rate mechanics that apply at the investor level. Pull statutory rates at write time with a source and date and label them `unverified` until confirmed — never carry a rate forward from a previous run, and never state any of this as advice. Most online material on ETF tax is written for US investors and is wrong here in ways that change the ranking of two otherwise identical funds.

8. **Write `funds/{TICKER}/ic-sheet.md`** per "IC sheet (canonical)", from the template in `funds/_TICKER/ic-sheet.md`. The fund variant's required sections differ from the company variant — no Business, no per-name Sizing — and the lint enforces the fund set, so do not import a company sheet's headings. Regenerate it whole on every run, source every line from the two documents just written (nothing originates here), and honour the style firewall: short declarative bullets, no em- or en-dashes anywhere in the file, every figure dated. The header table's fund-specific fields are the allocation verdict, ISIN and share class, index and variant, NAV with its date, the expected real return range with its horizon, the all-in annual drag, and the next dated event — which for a fund is the annual report, an index review, or a fee change, not an earnings date.

   **Variant perception on a fund is usually not a variant view on the asset.** For a broad index exposure the honest statement is often that we have no disagreement with the market about the asset at all, and that the entire edge is in cost, tax and holding behaviour — where it is knowable rather than forecast. Write that when it is true. Manufactured contrarianism is as dishonest as hidden divergence, and inventing a macro view to fill the section is the failure mode this section has on funds.

9. **Write back to whichever lens learned something.** Append a dated entry under "What I've learned over time" in the vehicle framework, the exposure framework, or both, tagged `- YYYY-MM-DD ({TICKER} fund deep-dive): {what was new}`. Route by the same test the tiers use: a finding about wrappers in general — a provider disclosure that turned out unreliable, a cost that only the annual report revealed, a structural quirk of a replication method — belongs in the **vehicle** framework even though it was discovered on one asset class, and that file compounds fastest because it is universal. A finding about what drives this asset class's returns belongs in the **exposure** framework.

   Honest negatives are written, not skipped: `- YYYY-MM-DD ({TICKER} fund deep-dive): no framework-level updates — the existing framework captured everything material.` If a lens is concurrently being written by a peer invocation, do not write inline: record it as an "Outstanding write-back" note in `thesis.md`, name the target file and section, and say so in the run's report, per "Concurrent orchestration (canonical)".

9b. **Write back to `principles/` — only what clears the tier test.** Per "Principles routing (canonical)", the sentence must survive with the ticker deleted *and* the industry deleted; for a fund, read that as the ticker, the provider **and** the asset class. "Accumulating wrappers defer tax" is not a principle, it is vehicle-framework content. "A cost you can know with certainty deserves more analytical effort than a return you can only estimate" survives every deletion and is a principle. Minting a new item or rewriting existing text follows "Tiered autowrite (canonical)". The honest negative is the expected outcome most of the time.

10. **Route the verdict.** A fund deep-dive ends in one of three routings:

    **ALLOCATE** — hand off to the user with the target weight, the band, and what it displaces. `portfolio.md` position rows are owner-only per "Tiered autowrite (canonical)"; if he acts, `decision-log` records it.

    **WATCH** — append to `watchlist.md` under "Researched, waiting for entry" with today's date, the expected-return range, and an explicit trigger. For a fund the trigger is **not a price**: it is an expected-return or valuation-percentile level, or a named vehicle event — a fee cut, the fund crossing a size threshold, an index methodology review, a share class launching in the form he actually wants. Write the row as `{TICKER} ({ISIN})` so it identifies one share class, and put the exposure slug where a company row carries its sector.

    **PASS** — a valid output, logged per PROD-P6 with a reason and a revisit condition. Do not delete the documents; they are the source material for the next revisit. Append `### YYYY-MM-DD — PASS` to the thesis Update log.

    **The routing that only exists for funds: right exposure, wrong vehicle.** That is a PASS on this ISIN plus a named substitute — the pass row must carry the alternative's ISIN and the reason it wins, otherwise the work is lost and the next run re-derives it. It is explicitly **not** a verdict on the asset class, and the thesis entry should say so in the same line.

## Output

Files written (`thesis.md`, `valuation.md`, `ic-sheet.md`), plus a verbal summary. The IC sheet is the summary's skeleton — if they diverge, one of them is wrong:

- What the exposure actually is, in three sentences, from the rulebook
- The expected real return range over the stated horizon, decomposed, with the drag and tax lines visible as separate terms
- The vehicle verdict, stated separately from the exposure view
- The best alternative vehicle on the same exposure, named with its ISIN, and why this one or that one wins
- The tax items to confirm with his advisor, and what changes in the ranking if one of them goes the other way
- The Principles check, rendered — every verdict line as written, plus the 1-2 most bearing attributed voices with their `holds when` conditions; or the sentinel verbatim, with the slugs reviewed
- The recommended allocation and band, what it displaces, and the drawdown the sizing assumes

## Notes

- **The wrapper is a certain cost against an uncertain return.** Every forecast in step 5 is an estimate; the ongoing charge is a fact, and it is deducted whether the market rises or falls. Twenty basis points of difference is twenty basis points a year, every year, and over a multi-decade hold it is one of the largest single numbers in the analysis — compute it on the owner's actual intended allocation and horizon and put that figure in the sheet. Spend the effort there. It is the part of this analysis you can actually know, and it is the part most write-ups skip in favour of a macro view they cannot know.
- **Two lenses, two verdicts** — per "Funds tier (canonical)". "Right exposure, wrong vehicle" is a common and actionable finding, and it is a fund switch rather than a decision about the asset class. Keep the two judgments in separate sentences from step 4 onward: the moment they blend, a fixable cost problem starts arguing about macro, and a wrapper that should have been swapped for a cheaper clone instead becomes an argument about whether to own the asset at all.
- **Backtested index history is not fund history.** An index computed backwards by its sponsor has no spread, no cash drag, no securities lending, no withholding, and no live redemption; it was also selected, at least in part, because the backtest looked good. Present live NAV history from the share class's own inception, say how short it is, and never let a simulated series stand where a track record is implied.
- **The most prominent number on the factsheet is the least useful one.** A fund with a great trailing 10-year return has, all else equal, a *worse* forward expected return, because a large share of that return came from multiple expansion that has already happened and is now the starting valuation in step 5. Trailing performance enters this analysis in exactly two ways: as an input to realized tracking difference, and as evidence about the *vehicle*. It never enters as evidence about the exposure.
- **The index committee is the active manager.** A rulebook picks, weights, caps and rebalances; "passive" describes the fee, not the absence of decisions. Read the methodology as you would read a mandate — including who can change it, and with how much notice.
- **Allocation, not conviction, is the output.** Sizing follows ASYM-P2's asymmetry basis as everywhere else — computed one way, off the canonical table in `valuation.md`, and stated. What changes here is which risk the ratio is standing in for. ASYM-P2's own `holds when` warns that the ratio treats a large drawdown and a permanent loss as the same thing scaled differently; a diversified fund is the case where the bear leg genuinely *is* a drawdown, so the binding constraint becomes the **drawdown distribution and time-to-recovery**, not permanent loss of a single business. Operationally: write the deepest historical drawdown and its recovery time into the allocation logic as numbers, and size so that outcome is one the owner can hold through and add into. A fund will not go to zero the way a single name can; it can stay down longer than a conviction lasts.
- **Tax is not an appendix and it is not advice.** It changes the ranking between two otherwise identical funds, so it belongs in the decomposition. Encode what to check and why it matters, label confidence honestly, and say plainly in the document that the specifics need his tax advisor's confirmation. Where a tax question stays open, the honest output is a wider range on that row and a named question, not a confident number.
- **"Could not establish" is a normal result here.** Securities-lending splits, the true cost of a swap, and the fee a market maker earns are often not disclosed anywhere. Say so, widen the range, and name the document that would close it — the same discipline `absorb` applies to a source it cannot fully read.
- **Do not manufacture a Principles check.** Several themes in `principles/` were minted from single-company analysis and genuinely do not bear on a wrapper; the capital-allocation theme has no allocator to judge. The sentinel is a complete answer, and it is the right one more often on funds than on companies.
- Append-only: every future update adds a dated entry. Never overwrite. The first entry is the record of what the allocation case originally was, and it is what a later post-mortem grades.
