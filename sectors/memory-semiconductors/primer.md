## TL;DR

**Memory is not one sector. It is two industries sharing a name, and the DRAM/NAND split — not the AI story — is the load-bearing structure.** DRAM has three players holding ~90% of revenue, a single-vendor lithography chokepoint (EUV), and an internal product that eats its own wafer supply (HBM consumes ~3x the wafer area per gigabit of DDR5, ~4x for HBM4, and was ~19% of DRAM wafer input in 2025 rising to ~22-23% in 2026e — TrendForce via trade reports, `partial`). NAND has six-plus players, no lithography bottleneck (it scales vertically with etch and deposition tools anyone can buy), no wafer-consuming valve, and a state-funded entrant with no return requirement. The cleanest possible confirmation arrived **today**: TrendForce, 2026-07-30, forecasts DRAM staying tight and *widening* into 2027 while NAND supply growth outpaces demand and prices face downward pressure (`verified` from the release text). If the AI demand shift were the structural fact, it would lift both. It lifts the one with the chokepoint.

**This cycle's demand story is genuinely the best on record, and that is orthogonal to whether the equities work from here.** Both are true and the sector work has to hold them simultaneously. Pricing momentum — the second derivative, which is what memory equities discount — has already rolled: DRAM contract prices went +43-48% QoQ (Q4'25) → +93-98% (Q1'26) → +58-63% (Q2'26) → +13-18%e (Q3'26); NAND went +55-60% (Q1'26) → +70-75% (Q2'26) → +10-15%e (Q3'26). **DRAM momentum peaked in Q1 2026 and NAND one quarter later** — the same lag the structural argument predicts. Meanwhile the supply response has been announced in public at peak margin, which is the industry's oldest tell: SK hynix guided 2026 capex **+50% to ≥$31bn on 2026-07-29** (`partial`) and its stock fell on the print.

**Every memory name in the world sits on a mid-single-digit forward P/E at the same moment.** SNDK 5.87x, MU 5.56x, SK hynix ~5.2x, Samsung ~5-7x (2026-07-30 for the first two; the Korean figures are commentary, `unverified`). That is not five independent cheapness signals — it is one consensus forecast of a peak-earnings year, priced five times. Micron peaked in May 2018 on a ~4-5x forward P/E and fell ~56% over seven months **while earnings were still rising** (`unverified`, commentary). VAL-T1 is the whole sector in one line: a low multiple on peak earnings and a high multiple on trough earnings are the same mistake.

**Position for the vault:** this is a sector to understand cold now and to own against a book-value anchor later, not to buy on a forward P/E. The complex is already >20% off June highs — SNDK -46%, MU -29%, WDC -30%, STX -24% from peaks all struck between 2026-06-18 and 2026-06-25 — but a 30-45% drawdown has not yet been a reliable top signal in this cycle (MU printed a -30% air pocket in March 2026 that fully recovered and made new highs). The historical peak-to-trough in memory is 6-8 quarters of fundamentals and -50% to -80% of price, and MU's July 2000 peak took **21.5 years** to regain. Buying a memory franchise at a cycle top has cost decades, not quarters — which is why any thesis here needs a written kill switch at the *thesis* level rather than a drawdown rule (ASYM-P1: exit only when the written thesis breaks).

## Industry structure

The general industry treatment lives in `../../frameworks/memory-semiconductors.md` — start with its **sub-sector navigator**, which triages what you are actually looking at before any multiple is applied. What is sector-specific and current as of 2026-07-30:

- **Concentration is the whole argument.** DRAM Q1 2026 revenue share: Samsung 38.5-38.6%, SK hynix 28.8%, Micron 22.4% — ~90% in three hands, with CXMT reportedly moving from ~3% to ~8% (`partial` for the top three via TrendForce/Omdia trade reports 2026-06-01/09; the CXMT figure is Counterpoint relayed via social post, `unverified`). NAND 2026: Samsung ~29%, SK hynix/Solidigm ~14%, Kioxia ~14%, YMTC ~13%, Micron ~13%, SanDisk ~13% (`partial`). **The top three in DRAM sum to ~90%; in NAND they sum to ~57%.** There is no share level at which a NAND player can act as a price-setter and there are three at which a DRAM player can.
- **NAND has been the asset conglomerates dispose of, repeatedly, for two decades.** Toshiba spun out Kioxia (2018). SK hynix bought Intel's NAND business for ~$8.8bn total (2020 agreement; $6.6bn Dec 2021, $2.2bn Mar 2025). Western Digital separated SanDisk in February 2025 and kept HDD. That is a revealed-preference datapoint about through-cycle returns that no margin series can give you.
- **The JV structure socialises the capex and privatises nothing.** SanDisk holds 49.9% of each of three Flash Ventures entities (Flash Partners, Flash Alliance, Flash Forward) spanning eight fabs in Japan; **Kioxia owns the buildings** and supplies wafer services at cost; each parent takes ~50% of output; and **SanDisk pays half of all fixed costs regardless of how much output it takes**, must fund ~50% of the JV's capex when JV operating cash flow is short, and is **contractually barred from manufacturing flash itself** (`verified`, SanDisk Form 10-Q q/e 2026-04-03 Note 10 and Form S-3ASR 2026-02-17). Read as an investor: a JV partner in NAND cannot cut its own fixed costs in a downturn. That is the opposite of the flexibility the equity holder needs at the trough — and it is also why the same structure levered gross margin from 22.5% to 78.4% in five quarters on the way up.
- **Long-term agreements are the genuinely new structural feature, and they cut both ways.** SanDisk disclosed **$41.6bn of remaining performance obligations at 2026-04-03, of which $41.2bn not yet billed and only ~15% expected as revenue within twelve months** (`verified`, 10-Q Note 4); Micron disclosed ~$100bn of customer agreements at Q3 FY26 (`partial`). But TrendForce's own explanation for why Q3 2026 server DRAM increases moderate to +13-18% is that **LTAs with US CSPs restrict suppliers from raising prices** (2026-07-09, `verified`). A supplier that sold forward at fixed price into a shortage has swapped the top of the cycle for the bottom of it. That trade may be correct. It must be priced, not assumed.
- **The market's own verdict on durability is visible in the HDD/memory multiple spread.** WDC trades at 27.8x forward and STX at 15.1x, against SNDK 5.84x and MU 5.56x (2026-07-30) — the market capitalises HDD earnings at three to five times the multiple it gives memory earnings, on businesses feeding the same AI-storage demand. That spread is not an arbitrage. It is the market stating it does not believe memory's current earnings repeat.

## What's changed in the last 5 years

- **The 2022-23 trough was the deepest on record and it re-based every balance sheet.** Micron revenue fell 49.5% in one fiscal year ($30.76bn → $15.54bn), GAAP net loss $5.83bn, one quarter at **-32.7% gross margin**, $1.83bn of inventory write-downs, FY2023 FCF **-$5.45bn** (`verified` for the annual figures from Micron's own releases; the quarterly detail is `partial`). SK hynix printed a **-67% operating margin** in Q1 2023 (`partial`). DRAM demand bit growth fell to **8.3%** — the first sub-10% year on record.
- **HBM was invented as a category and became DRAM's supply valve.** There is no analogue in any prior cycle and none in NAND: every incremental unit of AI DRAM demand *destroys* conventional DRAM bit supply because HBM consumes 3-4x the wafer per bit. HBM wafer capacity is growing ~29% YoY against ~10% for commodity DRAM (`partial`).
- **NAND was structurally disaggregated while DRAM consolidated.** Kioxia out of Toshiba (2018), Intel NAND into SK hynix (2021-2025), SanDisk out of Western Digital (2025-02-21; first trading bar 36.00 on 2025-02-13, when-issued). Consolidation kept getting *announced* in NAND (WDC/Kioxia) and not happening, while YMTC added a seventh player.
- **China arrived, and it does not need a return on capital.** CXMT DRAM capacity went ~100k wafer starts/month (early 2024) → ~290k (end 2025) → ~350k forecast end-2026, roughly 25k WSPM below Micron (`unverified`, trade press citing research firms). YMTC grew NAND revenue ~445% YoY in Q1 2026 and targets 15% shipment share by end-2026; Chinese suppliers are forecast at **~19% of global NAND bit output by 2027** (`verified` from the TrendForce release text, 2026-07-21).
- **Enterprise SSD overtook consumer in NAND market value in 2026** (`partial`) — the best structural thing that has happened to NAND in a decade, and it is a mix shift, not a moat.
- **Contract structure changed.** Multi-year hyperscaler LTAs with financial guarantees and customer prepayments are new to memory. SanDisk's contract liabilities went from $25M (2025-06-27) to **$511M** (2026-04-03) on customer advances (`verified`, 10-Q).

## What's likely to change in the next 5 years

Written as falsifiable pairs, because "structurally different" and "the usual cycle with a better story" make *different* observable predictions. This table is the sector's discriminating test and should be re-read at every review.

| If structurally different | If the usual cycle | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| DRAM sufficiency ratio stays negative through 2027 **after** the 2027-28 fab wave lands | Sufficiency flips positive in 2027, as TrendForce already forecasts for NAND | TrendForce quarterly sufficiency-ratio releases |
| Gross margins settle in a *new* higher band (70%+) at the next trough | Trough gross margin goes negative again, as in 2023 | The trough quarter's GAAP GM at MU and SNDK |
| Peak-to-trough revenue decline is 15-25% | -40%+, as in Micron's FY2023 -49.5% | Full-year revenue, peak year to trough year |
| Cost-per-bit decline stays faster than ASP decline through a down-leg | ASPs fall through cost, as they always have | Gross margin at a stated ASP change (no supplier discloses cost/bit) |
| Chinese capacity fails to qualify at tier-1 customers | Apple/hyperscalers qualify CXMT/YMTC parts | Qualification news; Apple reportedly testing CXMT parts late July 2026 (`unverified`) |
| LTA fixed-price floors hold through a down-quarter | Customers renegotiate or take the volume and refuse the price | The first down-quarter — **no NAND LTA has ever been stress-tested through a collapse** |

Additional calls where consensus may be wrong:

- **The capital constraint, not the physics constraint, is what breaks this industry.** The physics argument for DRAM is real — EUV is single-vendor and density gains have collapsed from ~100x/decade to ~2x/decade. But Samsung and SK hynix have announced >$500bn across four new memory fabs plus ~$52bn for HBM packaging (`unverified`), Micron has committed >$250bn of US investment through 2035 (`partial`), and greenfield DRAM runs $15-20bn+ and 2-3 years to first wafer (`unverified`). **The capex being announced today is the oversupply of 2028-2029.** Every "capital discipline is real this time" claim was killed in 1995-96 (~50 fabs announced) and 2017-18, and is being killed right now on the tape.
- **Demand destruction is already visible in the sellers' own numbers.** Smartphone production is forecast -15-20% YoY in 2026, notebooks ~-10%, servers +17% (TrendForce 2026-07-21). **Samsung's own MX/mobile division posted its first-ever operating loss (~KRW 0.7tn) in Q2 2026 because memory got too expensive** (`partial`). When the seller's own downstream division loses money on the price, the price is not stable.
- **A software or architecture answer to the shortage is the underpriced tail risk.** Google's "TurboQuant" KV-cache compression, reported 2026-03, claimed a ≥60% reduction in LLM memory footprint (one secondary report claims ~6x — the two figures conflict and **both are `unverified`; I could not reach a primary announcement**). This is VAL-T2 in its memory form: compression driven by cycle fear mean-reverts, compression driven by an architecture shift need not, and on a screen they look identical. Whoever writes a thesis here should verify it or write a kill switch keyed to it (PROD-P3 requires a named threshold, timeframe, and observable).
- **HBF (High Bandwidth Flash) is real optionality on a 2030 clock, not a 2027 revenue line.** SanDisk and SK hynix launched a standardisation consortium 2026-02-25 with an OCP workstream; reported targets ~1.6 TB/s read and 512GB stacks, first samples 2H 2026 (`partial`). Note the partner is a NAND competitor and SanDisk's Flash Ventures non-compete constrains where it can be fabricated.
- **The module and channel layer gets routed around.** Direct multi-year hyperscaler LTAs with prepayments and financial guarantees disintermediate the distributor tier that historically absorbed volatility. That removes the spot-market reflexivity that amplified prior busts — and removes a shock absorber.

## Where economic profit pools sit

Three claims, in order of how much I think each explains through-cycle returns:

1. **DRAM weighting is the highest-explanatory-power variable in the sector.** The cleanest available evidence is the same trough and the same recovery, two business mixes:

   | Fiscal year | **Micron** (DRAM ~76-80% of revenue) net income | **SanDisk** (pure NAND) net income |
   |---|---|---|
   | FY2023 | **-$5.83bn** (revenue $15.54bn, from $30.76bn) | **-$2.14bn** |
   | FY2024 | **+$0.778bn** (revenue $25.11bn) | **-$0.67bn** |
   | FY2025 | **+$8.54bn** (revenue $37.38bn) | **-$1.64bn** |

   Micron figures `verified` from its own annual releases (2023-09-27, 2024-09-25, 2025-09-23); SanDisk figures `verified` from its FY2025 10-K (period ended 2025-06-27, filed 2025-08-21). **The DRAM-weighted operator was back at record profit while the pure-NAND operator was still losing money two years later.** Caveat honestly: n=2, one cycle, mismatched fiscal calendars (~2 months), and SanDisk carried spin-off and impairment noise. It is illustration, not proof — and no clean through-cycle ROIC series comparing DRAM-weighted to NAND-only producers exists in public sources (see Open questions).

2. **Within a product, the profit accrues to cost-per-bit *rank*, not absolute cost** (MOAT-P4 — where output is undifferentiated the only durable advantage is the ability to earn at the price that forces the marginal producer out). The only question at the trough is whether you are above water when the highest-cost player is not. Layer count and node transitions matter exactly and only insofar as they convert into cost-per-bit rank.

3. **Across the chain, the profit sits in the fab layer during a shortage and migrates to equipment and to HDD substitutes during a glut.** See `value-chain.md` for the stage-by-stage split. The one durable structural pool is equipment — ASML has a single-vendor position in EUV that survives the memory cycle's amplitude with far less earnings volatility, which is the picks-and-shovels expression of the same demand without the price exposure.

Inside the current print, the absolute numbers are outside all precedent: Micron's fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended 2026-05-28) revenue **$41.46bn at 84.6% GAAP gross margin and 80.4% GAAP operating margin**, guiding Q4 to **$50.0bn ± $1.0bn at ~86% GM** (`partial` — issuer's own release read through a mirror; the SEC original returned HTTP 403). Micron's TTM revenue is roughly **3.0x its previous all-time cycle-peak year** (FY2018, $30.4bn) at a higher margin. Nothing in the historical record calibrates a cycle of this amplitude. That is a warning about the downside *and* an honest argument that the historical base rates may not bind cleanly.

## Open questions

- **Do the LTA price floors hold through a down-quarter?** The single highest-value unknown in the sector. SanDisk's $41.6bn RPO is `verified` as a *quantum*; whether the contracts fix price, fix a price floor, or fix volume only is **not disclosed in any filing read this session**. A volume-only commitment at spot price is not a floor, and a fifth of a fifth of consensus revenue is not a moat. Resolving observable: the first quarter in which spot rolls.
- **Where does SanDisk's contracted floor sit against consensus?** $42bn of minimum revenue over up to five years is roughly $8-10bn/year against FY2027 consensus revenue of $49.87bn — i.e. the contracted floor plausibly covers about a fifth of what consensus assumes, leaving ~four-fifths spot-exposed. **This arithmetic rests on secondary figures for the five-agreement total and on an assumption that the $42bn runs the full five years.** It is the highest-value verification item in the whole folder.
- **Is there a through-cycle ROIC or ROE series separating DRAM-weighted from NAND-only producers?** Searched directly; it does not exist in public secondary sources. The FY2023-25 net-income comparison above is the substitute, and it is n=2. Building the real series (segment operating margins for Micron DRAM vs NAND, Samsung/SK hynix memory sub-segments, 2010-2026) needs paid data. **Until it exists, the sector's central structural claim is mechanistically supported and quantitatively unproven.**
- **What is the cost-per-bit series?** Cost-per-bit and ASP-per-bit *are* memory gross margin, and no supplier discloses either in absolute terms — only sporadic QoQ percentage changes. Any KPI keyed to cost-per-bit needs the gross-margin-at-a-stated-ASP-change proxy or it is unusable.
- **Does the SK hynix US-listing prospectus exist, and what does it say?** An SEC EDGAR URL for a Form 424B4 attributed to SK hynix (CIK 0002120882) was located but returned HTTP 403 and was **not read**. If real, a fresh mandated prospectus from the #2 player in both DRAM and NAND is the single richest unabsorbed primary source available on this sector. It belongs on `../../sources.md`. Until then the US listing itself is `unverified`.
- **No SEC filing was read for any competitor.** Micron's FY2025 10-K, Samsung's and SK hynix's disclosures, and Kioxia's Japanese filings are all unread. Kioxia in particular is SanDisk's manufacturing co-dependency and the most important unverified input to the NAND supply-response question — its reported capex plan (~¥470bn/yr FY2026-28, +66% vs FY2025; Yokkaichi Fab7 and Kitakami Fab2 reportedly at ~50% utilisation) is entirely secondary. **Reported ~50% utilisation matters more than any new-fab announcement: enormous bit supply can be added by filling existing shells with tools, without breaking ground.**
- **How is the 2026-07-30 tape to be read?** SNDK fell 56.5% in 34 calendar days to 1,015.89 on 2026-07-29 and then rose 23.4% in a single session. The reference price of 1,249.44 is one day off a capitulation low. Any fair value written against it must say so.
