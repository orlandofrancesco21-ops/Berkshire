## Tier 3 — only if relevant

- **Silicon Motion (SIMO), Phison (8299.TW), Marvell (MRVL).** Merchant SSD controllers and firmware. Design-cycle revenue rather than bit revenue, so they survive the down-leg with far less amplitude. The closest thing NAND has to a qualification-based switching cost sits here.
- **Winbond, Macronix, Nanya's specialty lines.** NOR flash and niche/specialty memory. Cumulative NOR and SLC NAND contract prices rose **more than 100% in 1H 2026** (`partial`) — the same cycle, smaller and less liquid.
- **Netlist, Rambus.** Memory IP and litigation. A different risk shape entirely; mentioned so they are not mistaken for operating exposure.
- **SMART Modular, and the enterprise-SSD assemblers.** Top-5 enterprise SSD revenue was $18.46bn in Q1 2026, **+86% QoQ** (`partial`), and **2026 is the year enterprise SSD revenue overtakes consumer in total NAND market value** — the best structural development in NAND in a decade, and still a mix shift rather than a moat.
- **Emerging non-volatile memory (MRAM, ReRAM) and CXL-attached memory.** Not researched this session. Flagged as a long-dated architecture risk to both products, not assessed.

## Why HDD is a different business — and why the comparison keeps being made anyway

WDC and STX show up in every memory peer table and belong in almost none of them. The differences are structural, not cosmetic:

- **Different supply mechanics.** HDD capacity is added by heads and media, not by wafer starts. There is no lithography gate, no HBM valve, no layer-count race, and no equivalent of a fab landing all at once. Capacity is added incrementally and can be throttled incrementally — which is why HDD downturns are shallower and shorter than memory's.
- **Different structure.** Nearline HDD is a two-player-plus oligopoly that has been consolidating for twenty years, against six-plus players in NAND with a new state-funded entrant.
- **They are a *substitute*, not a peer.** HDD is the direct alternative for high-capacity, low-IOPS storage and is currently **benefiting** from NAND scarcity. Their fortunes are negatively correlated with NAND pricing at the margin, not positively.
- **WDC specifically has not been a SanDisk comparable since February 2025.** WDC kept HDD; SanDisk got the entire NAND franchise. Post-separation they are counterparties in a shared history, not comps.
- **The market says so itself, loudly.** WDC trades at **27.8x forward** and STX at **15.1x**, against SNDK **5.84x** and MU **5.56x** (2026-07-30). **The market capitalises HDD earnings at three to five times the multiple it gives memory earnings, on businesses feeding the same AI-storage demand.** That spread is not a mispricing to arbitrage. It is the market's explicit statement that it believes HDD's current earnings are repeatable and memory's are not — which is the same view this primer arrives at from the supply side.

**The true NAND peer set is Samsung, SK hynix/Solidigm, Kioxia, YMTC and Micron's NAND segment.** None of those is a US-listed pure play, which is exactly why SanDisk gets compared to two HDD companies instead. That convenience is the sector's most common analytical error.

## Notable recent corporate actions

- **Western Digital separated SanDisk, 2025-02-21.** Pro-rata distribution of 116,035,464 shares (80.1%) at one-third of a SNDK share per WDC share, record date 2025-02-12; WDC retained 28,827,787 (19.9%). SanDisk funded a **net $1.5bn distribution to WDC** with a $2.0bn seven-year Term Loan B — i.e. it was levered on the way out to pay the parent. Regular-way trading from 2025-02-24 (the 2025-02-13 bar at 36.00 is when-issued). All `verified`, FY2025 10-K.
- **WDC's stake wound down to zero.** 21,314,768 shares (14.6%) disposed 2025-06-06 via debt-for-equity exchange; the remaining 7,513,019 registered for resale on an S-3ASR filed 2026-02-17 (`verified`). Completion reportedly 2026-02-18 for ~$3.1bn with JPMorgan and Bank of America affiliates — **`partial`, not confirmed in a primary document.**
- **Flash Ventures extended, 2026-01-29.** Flash Alliance and Flash Partners pushed from 2029-12-31 to **2034-12-31**, so all three JVs now co-terminate. SanDisk concurrently agreed to pay Kioxia **$1.2bn over 2026-2029** under an "Agreement to Enhance Collaboration" for continued supply availability — real cash, no corresponding asset (`verified`, 10-Q Note 10).
- **SanDisk retired all long-term debt 2026-03-04**, settling the $2.0bn Term Loan B with cash and booking a $46M extinguishment loss; the $1.5bn revolver is undrawn to 2030-02-21. **$6.0bn buyback authorised 2026-04-30.** No dividend. **No equity issued since separation** — partly because the Tax Matters Agreement restricts share issuances to preserve the tax-free spin (duration not established; the TMA exhibit was not read), and partly because they demonstrably do not need to. All `verified` except the TMA duration.
- **SanDisk + SK hynix launched an HBF standardisation consortium, 2026-02-25**, with an Open Compute Project workstream; reported targets ~1.6 TB/s read and 512GB stacks, first samples 2H 2026 (`partial`). Optionality on a ~2030 clock, not a near-term revenue line — and note the partner is a NAND competitor.
- **Kioxia and SanDisk began 10th-generation 3D flash production at Kitakami Fab2, early July 2026** — reported 332-layer, **~59% higher bit density per unit area** and ~33% faster transfer vs the 218-layer generation (`partial`). The cost-down engine for FY2027 bits, and the price-down engine the moment the deficit closes.
- **Management note.** SanDisk's CEO **David Goeckeler is the former CEO of Western Digital** — the architect of the split now running the half he separated, and also Chairman of the board he reports to. CFO **Luis Visoso** has an unusually short-tenure record in the CFO seat (Palo Alto Networks eight months, Unity ~three years, before that CFO of AWS). The entire board joined at separation. The one-time "Launch Grant" PSUs granted 2025-05-09 pay a maximum 300% of target at a rolling 90-day average of **$105.91**; the stock is at **$1,249.44**, roughly **11.8x the maximum hurdle** — nothing improper, but the plan's entire dynamic range was consumed by a commodity price move inside about a year and it now provides zero incremental incentive. All `verified` from the DEF 14A filed 2025-10-07.

## Where to start

Ranked for `company-deep-dive`, with the reason:

1. **MU** — the DRAM-weighted business, the best disclosure, and the only long public series against which any cycle claim in this folder can be calibrated. Everything else is easier after this one.
2. **SNDK** — the purest NAND expression, the most extreme setup (-46% in five weeks, 3.67x forward book, flat bits), the most primary-source coverage already assembled, and a print on **2026-08-05** that will settle several open questions at once. **Do not write a valuation dated before that print without an explicit review trigger on it** — "Stale thesis (canonical)" clause (b) fires by construction.
3. **ASML.AS** — the structural moat in the chain, the reason the DRAM/NAND asymmetry exists, and the same demand with far less price exposure. The one name here that might survive a full-cycle quality screen.
4. **SK hynix** — best HBM exposure and the swing factor on supply, gated on obtaining the 424B4 prospectus.
5. **STX or WDC** — as the *substitute* trade and as the market's own stated view on earnings durability, not as memory comps.
6. **Kioxia** — only after its filings are readable. Until then it is an unverified input, not an investable name.

CXMT and YMTC are not investable here and belong on the monitoring list, not the deep-dive list.
